# Marpaul Southern Ltd v Betteshanger Sustainable Park Ltd (in creditors' voluntary liquidation) — [2022] EWHC 1515 (Ch)
- **Court / venue:** High Court of Justice, Business and Property Courts of England and Wales, Insolvency and Companies List (ChD), Birmingham Civil Justice Centre, 33 Bull Street, Birmingham (not a London venue). Case No CR-2021-BHM-000037
- **Judge:** His Honour Judge Williams (sitting as a Judge of the High Court)
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Ex tempore judgment, Monday 16 May 2022 (transcript by Epiq Europe Ltd); hearing dates not separately stated
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/ch/2022/1515
- **Tier:** borderline — a contractor's unpaid invoices and final account were proved against a debtor that disputed whether any contract existed and whether the invoices were substantiated. Caveat: this is an appeal against a liquidator's rejection of a proof of debt (r.14.8 Insolvency (England and Wales) Rules 2016) in a construction management contract, not an ordinary debt claim. Interest is not in issue, and actual recovery is limited to a dividend.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Applicant / creditor:** Marpaul Southern Ltd, management contractor that built the visitor centre and museum at Betteshanger Country Park [paras 1, 4, 6].
- **Respondent / debtor:** Betteshanger Sustainable Park Ltd, a special purpose vehicle wholly owned by Hadlow College, now in creditors' voluntary liquidation and acting by its joint liquidators [paras 1, 3, 12]. Hadlow College (in administration from 22 May 2019) is the parent and the alternative candidate for contracting party [paras 8-9].

### Amounts
- **Proof of debt:** £997,439.56 including VAT [para 13].
- **Final account:** entitlement £4,574,684 plus VAT; paid £3,876,200 plus VAT; balance claimed £698,484 plus VAT for works up to January 2019 [para 50].
- **Post-suspension invoices:** £17,516.25 plus VAT (securing the site) and £173,193.81 plus VAT (standing charges) [para 59].
- **Counterclaim / set-off:** liquidated damages were raised by the liquidators (Mr Green) as a possibility; no figure stated [para 58].
- **Interest claimed:** not stated.
- **Context:** estimated dividend to unsecured creditors of 16p in the pound, so Marpaul's maximum dividend was about £160,000; combined costs about £220,000 [para 18].

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **No contract with the Company:** the invoices were not addressed to the Company, and the 2015 engagement letter and the first statutory demand (April 2019) pointed to Hadlow College [paras 14, 30].
2. **JCT contract backdated:** allegedly created in May 2019, not on 19 August 2017 (solicitors' letter of 25 July 2019) [paras 10, 24].
3. **JCT contract a sham:** a paper exercise for funders, not followed in practice (no contract administrator, payment applications not made under Section 4) [para 30].
4. **Invoices unsubstantiated:** minimal descriptions (e.g. "Standing charge", £249,156); no underlying cost documents; no employer's QS approval; full JCT terms produced only in the fifth witness statement (10 March 2022) [paras 16, 51, 53].
5. **Liquidated damages** for late completion absent an extension of time [para 58].
6. **Post-January 2019 charges** were instructed by Hadlow College, not the Company [para 59].
7. **Procedural:** split trial application to defer quantum (13 April 2022) [para 16].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected.** The JCT contract formalised the relationship between Marpaul and the Company [paras 43, 57].
2. **Rejected.** The liquidators conceded in closing that there was insufficient evidence that the contract was not genuinely signed, after Mr Lumsdon-Taylor's evidence was found "utterly compelling" [para 29].
3. **Rejected.** A strong presumption applies against sham. The Supplemental Particulars matched the valuations (£6.882m; 25% OH&P and fee), departure from the terms does not show sham, and dishonesty was never put to the witness [paras 40-49].
4. **Rejected.** The fifth witness statement proved the account through contemporaneous monthly valuations. The Company chose to rely on Marpaul's QS and never challenged the valuations, so the liquidators were "seeking to move the goalposts" [paras 52-57]. The judge accepted that the initial rejection, based on bare invoices, could not be criticised [para 52].
5. **Rejected.** The January to August 2017 suspension was caused by the College's funding problems, the programme was re-baselined, there were significant variations, and no LD claim was ever raised [para 58].
6. **Upheld.** Mr Morley's emails of 14 and 29 March 2019 show the instructions were given for the College, outside the JCT contract [paras 60-62].
7. **Rejected.** The application was late, would add disproportionate cost and delay, and expert evidence was not reasonably required [paras 17-20].

### Money result
- Marpaul proved its final account; the unpaid balance as claimed (£698,484 plus VAT for works up to January 2019) is due from the Company under the JCT contract [paras 50, 57].
- The post-January 2019 claims (£17,516.25 plus VAT and £173,193.81 plus VAT) were not established against the Company [para 62].
- No liquidated damages set-off [para 58].
- Interest: not stated. Final admitted figure including VAT and form of order: not stated.
- Costs: Marpaul, as the overall successful party, gets its costs paid by the Company; the liquidators are entitled to an indemnity for their costs [para 64].

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* What decided quantum was the contemporaneous valuation trail (monthly valuations against a cost plan, never challenged) together with the debtor's own emails showing it relied on the creditor's QS. Bare invoices with one-line descriptions were not enough, and the judge said the liquidators could not be criticised for rejecting the proof on that basis [para 52].
- *Commentary:* Lesson for collections: prove the whole account (total value of work less total paid) rather than picking individual invoices, and address invoices to the correct contracting entity. Serving a statutory demand on the wrong group company first was used against the creditor.
- *Commentary:* Challenge should press "who is the contracting party" whenever invoices are addressed to a different group entity. Here it should uphold that point for work instructed by a different entity after the contract period (the Morley emails), but reject sham or backdating arguments without cogent evidence.
- *Commentary:* Reconciliation should compute the balance as final account value less payments (£4,574,684 less £3,876,200 = £698,484 plus VAT) and keep separately instructed work in its own bucket.
- *Commentary:* Resolution: in an insolvency setting the action is a substantiated proof of debt (or an appeal against rejection), not a letter before action. Expected recovery is a dividend (about 16p in the pound), so legal spend should be proportionate.
