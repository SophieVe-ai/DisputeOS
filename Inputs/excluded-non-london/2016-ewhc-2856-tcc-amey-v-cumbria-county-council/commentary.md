# Amey LG Ltd v Cumbria County Council — [2016] EWHC 2856 (TCC)
- **Court / venue:** High Court of Justice, Queen's Bench Division, Manchester District Registry, Technology and Construction Court, Manchester Civil Justice Centre, 1 Bridge Street West, Manchester (not a London venue). Case No 3MA500110
- **Judge:** His Honour Judge Stephen Davies, sitting as a Judge of the High Court
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Trial over 42 court days from 8 February to 27 May 2016; draft judgments circulated 6 September and 3 November 2016; further hearings 27 and 28 October and 10 November 2016; handed down 11 November 2016 [paras 1.8-1.9]
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/tcc/2016/2856
- **Tier:** borderline — a services contractor recovered sums the council had deducted or withheld from its final monthly payments against defect counterclaims that largely failed, with contractual interest at the Late Payment Act rate. Caveat: this is a very large seven-year highways maintenance final account with dozens of claim and counterclaim heads. Only the payment and withholding mechanics and the net result map cleanly onto an invoice dispute.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Claimant / creditor:** Amey LG Ltd, provider of highways maintenance and associated services for 7 years from 1 April 2005 (it took over the council's direct labour organisation) [para 1.2].
- **Defendant / debtor (counterclaimant):** Cumbria County Council, the client. Capita acted as overseeing organisation, assessing sums due, until January 2011 [paras 1.3-1.4].

### Amounts
- **Claim as pleaded:** over £30 million plus interest [para 1.5].
- **Counterclaim:** about £20 million, even after taking Cumbria's valuation of Amey's claims into account [para 1.5].
- **Deducted or withheld from the last three monthly payments:** pleaded by Cumbria at £4,197,361.37; Amey's figure for withholdings was £4,197,363.18 [paras 37.18, 37.26].
- **Interest claimed:** contractual, under cl 11.8 at the Late Payment Act rate (8.5% as pleaded), compounded monthly, on £4,382,372.57 from 9 June 2012 [para 37.19].

### Debtor's defences and complaints (Cumbria)
1. **Defective patching and surfacing (Schedules 2 and 3),** extrapolated from samples across the whole contract. First pleaded at a 95% confidence rate; at trial (day 2, February 2016) recast as "representative" sampling. Raised after the contract ended in March 2012 [paras 1.17, 1.22-1.25].
2. **Notified defects outstanding (Schedule 7)**, for which £448,677 had been withheld [paras 34.65-34.66].
3. **Street lighting overcharging (Schedule 5)** and the related set-off of £1,523,166 [paras 33.18, 37.23].
4. **Waste material left at depots (Schedule 8)**, £358,701.49 [para 35.1].
5. **Subcontractor uplifts (Schedule 13)**, £52,807.22 [para 36.1].
6. **Efficiency savings / additional services free of charge**, deducted (£558,890 for 2011/12) [paras 21.26, 37.23].
7. **Interest:** cl 11.8 does not apply to justified withholdings or to final account claims. Alternatively, the 1998 Act applies in full (simple interest, with remission under s.5). Alternatively, nothing was due until judgment because Amey failed to provide verifying information [paras 37.13, 37.15, 37.32, 37.37].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected.** The extrapolated claims failed ("Cumbria's case fails on extrapolation"). Because they were pleaded solely as extrapolated claims, "failure on extrapolation means total failure", and an unpleaded fall-back on the samples was not allowed [paras 1.33, 25.168].
2. **Partly upheld.** Cumbria recovered £179,255.53, valued at Amey's rates rather than framework contractor rates [paras 34.64-34.65].
3. **Partly upheld.** Cumbria succeeded on three items; the agreed deduction is £92,691.80 [para 33.18].
4. **Partly upheld.** Cumbria was awarded 75% of the experts' agreed full value, £269,026.12 [para 35.17].
5. **Rejected.** "Cumbria has not made out its case" [para 36.13].
6. **Partly upheld / outcome mixed.** Cumbria made sufficient requests for free additional services in 2008/09 and 2009/10 [para 21.26]. However, the final payment for efficiency savings was due to Amey as a return of a sum "wrongfully deducted or withheld" [para 37.1]. The exact split is not stated in the passages reviewed.
7. **Rejected.** Cl 11.8 applies to any sum not paid when due, including set-offs against bona fide disputed sums and withholdings later found unjustified, and to annual and final accounts. Three deductions (January, February and March 2012) triggered the Late Payment Act rate on all unpaid sums. Only the rate, not the whole Act, is imported, so interest is compounded monthly and there is no s.5 remission. Cumbria's failure to assess was due to its own belief that Amey owed it money [paras 37.14, 37.16, 37.27-37.36, 37.38].

### Money result
- **Principal awarded to Amey:** £3,698,064.04 net of all counterclaims [para 37.1].
- **Interest to 9 November 2016:** £1,697,246.30, under cl 11.8 at the Late Payment Act rate (8% over base) compounded monthly from 9 June 2012 [paras 37.2, 37.29-37.31].
- **Total payable at 9 November 2016:** £5,365,093.34, including principal and interest on the separately settled winter services claim. Interest continues at £1,219.13 per day until payment [para 37.2].
- Amey is the net beneficiary only because Cumbria had deducted sums from the final three payments. Without those deductions Cumbria would have been the net beneficiary [para 37.5].
- Figures were agreed by the quantum experts on 9 November 2016 after the judge's rulings [paras 1.9, 37.1]. Costs: not stated.

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* What decided the money result was the payment mechanism. Cumbria deducted assessed sums from the final three certificates on the strength of counterclaims that largely failed. The bespoke cl 11.8 then converted three missed payments into Late Payment Act-rate compound interest on everything outstanding, adding about £1.7m to a principal of about £3.7m.
- *Commentary:* What decided the counterclaims was evidence quality. Defect claims built on non-random samples and extrapolated across the whole contract failed completely, because no alternative claim based on the inspected samples alone had been pleaded.
- *Commentary:* Challenge should not accept a debtor's set-off or withholding at face value. It should check whether a compliant withholding notice was given (amount and grounds, in time) and whether the cross-claim is proved item by item, not extrapolated from a sample. Defences 1 and 5 should be rejected; defences 2 to 4 are worth only their proven, rate-correct values.
- *Commentary:* Reconciliation should track missed payments individually, because a contractual escalation clause (here three missed payments) can change the interest rate and basis for all outstanding sums. It should apply contractual compound interest where the contract provides for it, rather than defaulting to simple statutory interest.
- *Commentary:* Resolution: where the contract has its own late-payment interest clause, the letter before action and court-claim pack should claim contractual interest under that clause, at the escalated rate and compounded as the contract provides, rather than statutory interest under the 1998 Act. Resolution should also flag the debtor's exposure to that interest as leverage for settlement.
