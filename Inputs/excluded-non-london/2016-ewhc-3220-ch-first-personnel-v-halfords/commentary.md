# First Personnel Services Ltd v Halfords Ltd — [2016] EWHC 3220 (Ch)
- **Court / venue:** High Court, Chancery Division, Birmingham District Registry (Case No A30BM337); judgment handed down at the Rolls Building, Fetter Lane, London EC4A 1NL
- **Judge:** Mr Jeremy Cousins QC (sitting as a Deputy Judge of the Chancery Division)
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Hearing 14, 15, 16, 20, 21, 22, 23, 27 June and 18, 19, 25 July 2016; handed down Tuesday 20 December 2016
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/ch/2016/3220
- **Tier:** core: staffing agency sues a retail client for admitted unpaid trading invoices and disputed transfer fees on loss of the contract; the client withholds everything on the strength of an overcharging counterclaim, which fails. Also a key authority on a 2% per month interest clause being struck down as a penalty and on remission of Late Payment Act interest for creditor delay.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Creditor / claimant:** First Personnel Services Ltd ("FPS"), recruitment agency supplying temporary workers to industrial and retail clients [para 1].
- **Debtor / defendant (counterclaimant):** Halfords Ltd, cycling and motoring retailer, client of FPS from the mid-1980s to December 2011 [para 1].
- **Non-party:** Staffline Group plc, replacement agency to which most workers transferred under TUPE on 22 December 2011 [para 3].

### Amounts
- **Unpaid trading invoices:** £68,211.44 [paras 4, 157].
- **Transfer fees claimed:** originally £557,789.94 inc VAT; recalculated £490,370.99 inc VAT (removing double-counted 12.07% holiday uplift); alternatives £442,372.41 (annualised pre-transfer hours) or £205,762.65 (post-transfer) [paras 4, 148, 149].
- **Halfords' figures:** at most about £21,000 (guaranteed hours cap); or £76,365.26 (actual hours year after transfer) [paras 4, 152].
- **Counterclaim:** overcharging of holiday pay, holiday pay on overtime and NI, said to exceed £500,000 (quantum not tried); plus £69,069.79 accrued holiday pay paid to Staffline [paras 5, 236].
- **Interest claimed:** contractual 2% per month (cl 4(i)); alternatively Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 or s 35A Senior Courts Act 1981 [paras 4, 159].

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **Conditions do not apply to a TUPE transfer between agencies** [para 4].
2. **Transfer-fee clauses onerous and unusual, so not incorporated** [para 4].
3. **Unenforceable under reg 10 of the Conduct of Employment Agencies and Employment Businesses Regulations 2003** [para 4].
4. **Void for uncertainty**, and any fee capped by Staffline's guaranteed hours (about £21,000) [paras 4, 127].
5. **Quantum:** only workers actually retained count (about 165, not 190); credits for 27 workers who left within six weeks and under cl 6(vii); fee base should be the year after transfer [paras 131, 139, 152].
6. **Set-off of overcharging counterclaim:** rates were cost-plus, so FPS overcharged holiday pay, NI (travel and subsistence scheme) and WTD on overtime; alternatively misrepresentation, negligent misstatement and Quistclose trust. Raised first around October 2011, during the retender [paras 5, 232, 234 to 236, 243].
7. **Double payment of holiday pay** (£69,069.79 paid to Staffline) [paras 237 to 239].
8. **Interest:** cl 4(i) limited to wage invoices and penal; any statutory interest should be remitted under s 5 of the 1998 Act for doubt created by FPS's drafting, unreliable information and 19 months' delay [paras 160, 161, 164].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected:** a fee is payable under cl 10 on a TUPE transfer between agencies [para 100].
2. **Rejected:** not onerous or unusual; on the front page; brought to Halfords' attention over years; widespread in the industry [paras 102 to 106].
3. **Rejected** for cl 6 and 10 read together (cl 6(i) alone would not have complied) [paras 109, 116].
4. **Rejected:** fee is ascertainable through IPS cl 3.4, 3.5, 3.9; guaranteed hours principle not in play [paras 126 to 130].
5. **Partly upheld:** claim limited to 165 retained workers on the January 2012 timesheets; credits rejected for unreliable evidence and cl 6(vii) inapplicable; base year is the year before transfer, using FPS's £442,372.41 methodology [paras 137, 138, 143 to 145, 153, 154].
6. **Rejected:** rates were composite, not cost-plus; no warranty, reliance, duty of care or Quistclose trust; counterclaim dismissed [paras 231 to 236, 244 to 246, 248].
7. **Rejected:** FPS had no contractual duty to Halfords to pay holiday pay and Halfords had no liability to Staffline [paras 240 to 242].
8. **Partly upheld:** cl 4(i) applies only to wage invoices and the 2% per month rate is a penalty (extravagant, no justification); 1998 Act interest awarded on all sums; drafting and information objections rejected; 12 of the 19 months of delay unreasonable, so the rate is halved for that period [paras 161, 163, 165 to 169].

### Money result
- £68,211.44 trading invoices: payable, no set-off since the counterclaim fails [paras 157, 248].
- Transfer fees: £442,372.41 methodology (annualised pre-transfer hours, actual pay rates plus 12.07% WTD), adjusted to 165 workers; final figure left to the parties [paras 154, 156, 247].
- Interest: Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 at 8% over official Bank Rate, reduced to 4% for a 12-month period of unreasonable delay; start date and computation left to be agreed or argued [paras 169, 247].
- Counterclaim dismissed [para 248]. Costs: not stated; consequential hearing to be listed [para 250].

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* What decided the claim was the client's admission that £68,211.44 was due combined with a failed counterclaim. Withholding undisputed invoices against a speculative cross-claim exposed Halfords to statutory interest on everything; the court quoted Ruttle that a payer must pay what is undisputed and ask for substantiation of the rest [para 166].
- *Commentary:* Collections lesson: a very high contractual interest rate (2% per month) can be struck down as a penalty without evidence justifying it; a creditor may end up with the 1998 Act rate, and it can be cut further for its own litigation delay. Act promptly and keep correspondence moving.
- *Commentary:* Challenge should reject set-off arguments where the debtor's overcharging theory contradicts how the parties actually priced (composite rates negotiated on the total), but should uphold quantum challenges supported by objective records (timesheets showing 25 workers not retained).
- *Commentary:* Reconciliation should separate admitted trading invoices from disputed fees, pro-rate transfer fees to proven headcount (165 of 190), and compute 1998 Act interest with a reduced-rate window where delay is attributable to the creditor.
- *Commentary:* Resolution: for admitted sums, a letter before action with statutory interest; for disputed fees, a settlement offer anchored to the court-approved methodology. DisputeOS should warn when a contractual interest rate is far above the statutory rate and default to the 1998 Act basis.
