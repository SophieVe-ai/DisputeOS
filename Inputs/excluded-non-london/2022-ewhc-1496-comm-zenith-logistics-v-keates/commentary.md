# Zenith Logistics Services (UK) Ltd and others v Keates and others — [2022] EWHC 1496 (Comm)
- **Court / venue:** High Court of Justice, Business and Property Courts in Wales, Circuit Commercial Court (QBD), Cardiff Civil Justice Centre, 2 Park Street, Cardiff CF10 1ET (Case No CC-2019-CDF-000006). The claims were commenced in London and transferred to Cardiff for trial [para 1]; no London trial venue.
- **Judge:** His Honour Judge Keyser QC, sitting as a Judge of the High Court
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Hearing 9, 10, 11, 14, 15, 16 and 23 March 2022; judgment handed down remotely Friday 17 June 2022
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/comm/2022/1496
- **Tier:** borderline; the relevant part is the "Biosol Claim", a haulier's claim on unpaid open-book invoices (GBP 260,992.75). Caveat: it sits inside a much larger director-fraud and conspiracy trial (the "Main Claim", over GBP 3m), and the trial was in Cardiff, not London. This commentary covers the Biosol Claim only.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Creditor (First Claimant):** Zenith Logistics Services (UK) Ltd ("Zenith"), a transport and logistics provider in the Uniserve Group [para 3].
- **Debtor (Eighth Defendant):** Biosol Renewables UK Ltd ("Biosol"), a seller of biomass boilers and woodchip that bought woodchip haulage services from Zenith; its director is Mr Neil Bundock (Seventh Defendant) [para 14].
- Context: Zenith's former MD, Mr Keates (First Defendant), made the contract for Zenith and was later found to have dishonestly misused Zenith's assets; Spring Logistics (Ninth Defendant) was his company [paras 4-5, 15].

### Amounts
- Claimed: GBP 260,992.75 unpaid invoices plus contractual interest (rate not stated) [para 129].
- Debtor's admission: qualified admission of GBP 46,000 (GBP 42,943.66 accepted on 11 Apr 2018) [paras 109, 130].
- Disputed items: driver rate (Biosol said GBP 75/day, not GBP 135), parking GBP 65/week, flatbed trailer use, low-loader charges (lease at GBP 798.10/month), insurance [paras 69, 147-151].
- No counterclaim by Biosol is described.

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **Contract terms**: haulage only "as and when required", paying only for vehicles on days used, not a dedicated fleet. Raised in substance only after the relationship broke down; no written challenge to the 3 Apr 2017 email or the 17 May 2017 email [paras 135-136].
2. **Termination by mutual agreement in October 2017**: from 27 Oct 2017 transport was said to be provided by Spring Logistics for Short Bros, so later invoices were not Biosol's liability. First raised in writing on 11-14 Apr 2018, after six months of continued invoicing and the 12 Mar 2018 promise to pay [paras 109, 130, 138].
3. **Invoices "repeatedly challenged" orally and credit notes promised** [para 136(7)].
4. **Open-book costs not proved by underlying documents** [para 146].
5. **Specific line items**: driver day rate, parking charge, flatbed trailer, low-loader trailer, insurance [paras 147-151].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected.** The 3 Apr 2017 email was the best evidence of the terms. Biosol never challenged it or the 17 May 2017 email in writing, and purpose-built dedicated trailers made a pay-per-use deal commercially implausible. Zenith could recover the costs of the dedicated vehicles and drivers, with credit for any revenue from other use [paras 134-137].
2. **Rejected.** The documents contain no mention of termination; Biosol never protested at the continued invoicing and payment demands; and Spring Logistics had no transport capacity, so its interposition made no sense [paras 142-144].
3. **Rejected.** The judge "unhesitatingly" rejected the evidence of oral challenges and found no challenge until after Mr Newnes' death (11 Mar 2018) and Mr Gambold's involvement [paras 136(2), 136(7)].
4. **Rejected.** Under an open-book contract the debtor could ask to see the underlying documents but never did. Itemised, unchallenged invoices were themselves evidence that the costs were incurred [paras 145-146].
5. **Partly upheld.** Driver rate GBP 135/day accepted [para 147]; parking charge allowed as a necessary overhead [para 148]; flatbed charges allowed given Biosol's inconsistent position [para 149]; insurance challenge failed [para 151]. **Low-loader charges upheld:** the trailer was probably kept at Mr Keates' farm and not used on the Biosol contract, so those charges were deducted [para 150].

### Money result
- Judgment for Zenith for the full amount claimed (GBP 260,992.75) less the low-loader trailer charges [paras 152, 310(1)]. The amount of the low-loader deduction, and therefore the net figure, is not stated.
- Contractual interest was claimed; rate and amount not stated. The precise terms of the order and all consequential matters (including costs) were left to a further hearing [para 311].

## Commentary
*(Commentary: author's analysis, not the court's.)*
- What decided the debt claim was the contemporaneous paper trail against the debtor's later oral account. A written summary of the deal that invited comment and got none; itemised open-book invoices sent from 4 Jul 2017 without challenge; a statement of account on 3 Jan 2018; and the debtor's own written promise to pay on 12 Mar 2018 outweighed claims of oral objections and a mutual termination that appeared nowhere in writing.
- The creditor still lost one line item: charges for a low loader that was not actually used for the debtor, because the creditor's own MD had misled its billing staff. Collections teams should check that each charged asset was actually deployed for the customer, especially where an insider set up the arrangement.
- An open-book contract does not require the creditor to prove every cost from source documents when the debtor never asked to inspect them. Unchallenged itemised invoices carry real evidential weight.
- DisputeOS mapping: Challenge should rate "we disputed it orally" and "credit notes were promised" as weak where there is no written query before the debt was chased, and especially where the debtor's later written promise to pay acknowledges the debt. It should test the alleged October 2017 termination against invoices continuing to be issued without protest. Reconciliation should rebuild the account from the weekly open-book spreadsheet, credit any revenue from other use of dedicated vehicles, and remove charges for assets not deployed for the customer (the low loader). Resolution: a court-claim pack for the balance with contractual interest; a Part 36 or settlement offer pitched at the full amount less the low-loader charges would have closely matched the result.
