# CIS General Insurance Ltd v IBM United Kingdom Ltd — [2021] EWHC 347 (TCC)
- **Court / venue:** High Court of Justice, Business and Property Courts of England and Wales, Technology and Construction Court (QBD), Royal Courts of Justice, Strand, London (Case No HT-2018-000154)
- **Judge:** Mrs Justice O'Farrell DBE
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Reading days 14 to 16 January 2020; hearing 20 January to 19 March 2020; written closings 8, 9, 27 and 28 April 2020; handed down remotely 19 February 2021
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/tcc/2021/347
- **Tier:** borderline — a supplier's unpaid milestone invoice (£2,889,600, AG5) was held payable, and the customer's dispute-notice and set-off timing was analysed in detail. Caveat: the invoice is a small counterclaim within a £128m IT project failure claim. The main issue is wrongful termination for non-payment, and the supplier ends up a net payer.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Claimant / customer (debtor on the invoice):** CIS General Insurance Ltd (CISGIL), a home and motor insurer, then a subsidiary of the Co-operative Group [para 2].
- **Defendant / supplier (creditor on the invoice; counterclaimant):** IBM United Kingdom Ltd, contracted to supply and implement a new IT platform ("Project Cobalt") and manage it for ten years [para 4]. Subcontractor: Innovation Group (IG), supplier of the "Insurer Suite" software [para 6].

### Amounts
- **Invoice (IBM counterclaim):** £2,889,600 [para 25]. Paid: nil.
- **CISGIL's claims:** wasted costs £128m (termination claim); alternatively £70.4m (warranty), £128m or £36.1m (reporting), £27.2m (delay costs) [paras 16-19]. IBM accepted £29.1m was wasted expenditure but relied on exclusions and a cap of £15.7m [para 24].
- **Interest claimed:** not stated in the passages on the invoice. The basis of interest on CISGIL's award was left to a consequentials hearing [paras 753-754].

### Debtor's defences and complaints (CISGIL's grounds for not paying AG5)
1. **Milestone not due:** AG5 was sequenced after Release 1 and 2 go-live and milestones IMP-021 to IMP-029, none of which had been achieved, and CISGIL had not approved the milestone (raised in the 27 March 2017 rejection and in the 12 May 2017 letter) [paras 10, 170].
2. **Invalid invoice:** no PO number, incorrect milestone description ("IMP-020"), SOW name missing, not submitted by the agreed procedure (first articulated 12 May 2017, about seven weeks after the invoice) [paras 170, 315].
3. **Validly disputed:** the 27 March 2017 email (two days after submission) was a dispute notice within 7 Business Days, so IBM could not serve a Final Notice or terminate [paras 11, 330].
4. **Equitable set-off** of CISGIL's damages for IBM's delay, notified 13 April 2017 (reservation), then 12 May, 8 June and 4 July 2017 [paras 166, 169, 331].
5. **Late termination:** IBM had to terminate by 13 July 2017 (15 business days after the Final Notice) or lose the right [para 363].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected.** On the natural meaning of the Roadmap and the SOW, the AG5 payment fell due in January 2017. CISGIL was obliged to approve the milestone and cannot rely on its own wrongful withholding of approval (Alghussein v Eton College) [paras 267, 278-280, 400(i)].
2. **Rejected.** The invoice was correctly prepared and properly submitted (e-billing had been agreed). CISGIL could not rely on the missing PO number because it had wrongfully withheld the PO, and these complaints "were not articulated at the time" and "have no merit" [paras 309, 315, 400(ii)].
3. **Upheld.** The 27 March 2017 email was a valid good-faith dispute under para 11.11. There was no obligation to give reasons in advance or to start the dispute procedure. So IBM could not use cl 26.7, and its termination was a repudiation that CISGIL accepted [paras 329-330, 364, 400(iii), (v), (vi)].
4. **Rejected.** Schedule 5 imposed "pay now, argue later": set-off against an invoice required a dispute notice within 7 Business Days, and the later set-off letters did not comply. CISGIL kept its right to counterclaim [paras 356-358, 400(iv)].
5. **Rejected.** Cl 26.7 sets a minimum period, not a deadline; termination 14 days after the right arose was reasonable [para 363].

### Money result
- IBM is entitled to payment of the AG5 invoice, **£2,889,600** [para 752].
- CISGIL recovers **£15,887,990** (subject to any applicable VAT reduction) for additional costs caused by IBM's delay and reporting breaches. Its wasted-expenditure claims are excluded by cl 23.3 of the MSA, and the warranty and "true state" reporting claims are dismissed [para 753(iv)-(ix)].
- IBM's £2,889,600 is set off against CISGIL's award, leaving a **net £12,998,390 payable to CISGIL** (subject to VAT reduction), with interest on that net sum [para 753(x)-(xi)].
- Interest rate and basis, costs and permission to appeal were left to an adjourned consequentials hearing [para 754].

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* The invoice itself was a winner for the supplier. The milestone date in the Roadmap was decisive, and the customer could not hide behind a PO it had wrongly refused to issue. What cost the supplier everything was escalating to termination over an invoice that had been disputed in time, even though the dispute was wrong on the merits.
- *Commentary:* Lesson for collections: a timely good-faith dispute notice (here two days after the invoice) can block contractual termination or suspension remedies even if the dispute later fails. Collect through the dispute procedure or in court, not through termination.
- *Commentary:* Challenge should separate (a) whether the debt is due, (b) whether the invoice was disputed within the contractual window, and (c) whether set-off was notified in time. Here Challenge should reject defences 1, 2, 4 and 5 but uphold 3 as a bar to termination. It should also give little weight to formal invoice defects raised for the first time weeks later.
- *Commentary:* Reconciliation should track the contractual clocks: 7 Business Days for payment and dispute, 180 days to invoice, 15 Business Days after a Final Notice. It should net the supplier's invoice against any established cross-claim, as the court did here.
- *Commentary:* Resolution: for a disputed milestone invoice, propose the dispute-resolution route or a court-claim pack for the invoice plus interest, and warn against termination. Where the customer has large cross-claims, a settlement offer that reflects the likely net position may be better than pursuing the invoice alone.
