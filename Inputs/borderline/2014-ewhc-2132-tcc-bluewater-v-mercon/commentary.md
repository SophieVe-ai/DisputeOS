# Bluewater Energy Services BV v Mercon Steel Structures BV and others — [2014] EWHC 2132 (TCC)
- **Court / venue:** High Court of Justice, Queen's Bench Division, Technology and Construction Court, Royal Courts of Justice, Strand, London (Case No HT-10-355; issued in the Commercial Court on 3 November 2009, transferred to the TCC on 29 November 2010)
- **Judge:** The Hon Mr Justice Ramsey
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Hearing dates not stated; judgment dated 30 June 2014
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/tcc/2014/2132
- **Tier:** borderline — a subcontractor's final account (lump sum, re-measured and reimbursable invoices, variation requests) was valued line by line against the employer's abatements, liquidated damages and termination damages. Caveat: this is a large offshore fabrication final-account dispute with termination. The "creditor" subcontractor ended up owing money, and interest is not addressed.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Claimant / employer (debtor on the final account):** Bluewater Energy Services BV, which ordered a tower-based soft yoke mooring system (SYMS) for Lukoil's Yuri Korchagin Field in the Caspian Sea [paras 1-2].
- **Defendants / subcontractor (creditor on the final account; counterclaimant):** Mercon Steel Structures BV (fabricator), with Mercon Holding BV and Mercon Groep BV joined on 24 June 2010 under parent company guarantees [paras 1, 13]. Mercon subcontracted assembly and integration to ASPO in Astrakhan [para 3].

### Amounts
- **Original contract price:** €10,468,233.37 [para 1535].
- **Total Contract Price contended:** Bluewater €17,912,436.66; Mercon €25,899,076.36 [para 519].
- **Paid (net, after the performance bond):** €20,086,403.71 [para 1535].
- **Bluewater's counterclaims:** abatements, re-measure discount, NCRs, pre- and post-termination damages, costs to complete, RFVAT, LDs for late delivery (up to the 7% cap) and LDs for key personnel [paras 20, 1535].
- **Mercon's further claims:** loss of profit on termination; unpaid RFVAT [paras 20, 1524, 1532].
- **Interest claimed:** not stated.

### Debtor's defences and complaints (Bluewater against Mercon's account)
1. **Valid termination for default** under Cl 30 (Notice of Default 23 January 2009; termination 3 February 2009), so no lost profit for Mercon [paras 12, 303].
2. **No extension of time**, so LDs are due on Key Dates B4.1 to B4.3, C3, C6 and C9 [paras 512, 519].
3. **Notice time-bar:** variation claims were forfeited for non-compliance with Cl 14.3 to 14.7 [para 981].
4. **VO-008 binding** as to most re-measured items [para 650].
5. **Abatement** for lump sum work not done or partly done [para 20].
6. **Reimbursable costs** recoverable only if actually incurred and paid to ASPO [para 800].
7. **Defects (NCRs), costs to complete, RFVAT and key personnel LDs** [para 20].
8. **Loss of profit excluded** by Cl 25 in any event [para 1524].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Upheld.** Bluewater validly terminated under Cl 30. Mercon was in default and failed to act on even minor items after the 23 January 2009 notice [paras 296-303, 1533].
2. **Upheld.** Mercon was not entitled to any extension of time. There was a contractual extension mechanism (so time was not at large) and no acts of prevention by Bluewater [paras 512, 515-516]. LDs for late delivery were awarded at €1,596,909.70, the 7% cap on a Total Contract Price of €22,812,997.76 [para 1535].
3. **Rejected as a blanket defence.** The forfeiture is discretionary and must not be exercised arbitrarily. Mere lateness or missing information is not enough, and Bluewater never justified any exercise of the discretion [paras 1009-1012]. Individual VORs were then valued on their merits [para 1013 onwards].
4. **Rejected.** VO-008 was an interim rolling re-measurement and not binding until finally agreed [para 653].
5. **Partly upheld.** Abatements of €71,581.36 (not done) and €15,385.00 (partly done) [para 1535].
6. **Upheld (conceded).** Mercon accepted it can recover only invoices actually paid to ASPO [para 800].
7. **Partly upheld.** NCRs €44,489.60; pre-termination damages €103,710.00; post-termination damages €743,556.63; costs to complete €215,365.60; RFVAT €372,563.05; key personnel LDs €150,000.00 [paras 1523, 1535].
8. **Upheld.** Cl 25's own definition clearly covers loss of profit from wrongful termination, so Mercon's lost profit claim would fail even if termination had been wrongful [paras 1524, 1531].

### Money result (final account table, para 1535)
- Revised Total Contract Price €22,812,997.76: agreed part €7,166,090.75 plus determined Mercon claims €15,646,907.01. These include VO-008 re-measure €12,860,826.03, reimbursable work €1,075,965.67, mark-up €88,896.49, Star Rates €454,899.87, VO-003 €89,200.00 and VORs €1,077,118.95.
- Less payments €20,086,403.71; less Bluewater claims €3,687,918.71 (including VO-008 discount €374,357.77).
- **Net balance due to Bluewater: €961,324.66.**
- Interest, costs and the form of order: not stated.

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* What decided the case was contemporaneous site evidence on the defaults listed in the 23 January 2009 notice, together with Mercon's failure to act on even minor items. That justified termination and, with no extension of time, triggered LDs to the cap. The LDs alone (€1.6m) swung the net position against the subcontractor.
- *Commentary:* Lesson for collections: a supplier's unpaid final account can be wiped out by liquidated damages and termination costs where it cannot prove entitlement to more time. Delay events and notices need to be documented as they happen. On the other hand, a discretionary notice time-bar cannot be used mechanically to refuse payment for instructed extra work.
- *Commentary:* Challenge should test the employer's set-offs one by one (LDs, abatement, NCRs, completion costs). It should reject a blanket time-bar where the clause gives the payer a discretion it has not genuinely exercised, and treat "interim" signed valuations as revisable.
- *Commentary:* Reconciliation should build the account in the court's format: agreed contract sum, plus determined variations and re-measure, less payments (including settlement monies and any bond drawn), less established cross-claims. It should cap LDs at the contractual percentage of the revised Total Contract Price.
- *Commentary:* Resolution: on these facts the "creditor" subcontractor should not issue a letter before action. The correct output is a net-liability assessment, or a settlement offer reflecting the LD exposure.
