# E-Nik Ltd v Department for Communities and Local Government — [2012] EWHC 3027 (Comm)
- **Court / venue:** High Court of Justice, Queen's Bench Division, Commercial Court, Royal Courts of Justice, Strand, London (Case No 2012 Folio 745)
- **Judge:** Mr Justice Burton
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Hearing 12 October 2012; judgment dated 2 November 2012
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/comm/2012/3027
- **Tier:** borderline — two unpaid invoices under an IT consultancy contract were recovered as a debt with statutory interest under the 1998 Act; caveat: the debt is a minimum-purchase ("take or pay" style) commitment for services made available rather than services actually delivered, and it was decided on cross-applications for summary judgment, not after trial.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Claimant / creditor:** E-Nik Ltd, an information technology services company supplying consultancy services (the "Supplier") [para 1].
- **Defendant / debtor:** Department for Communities and Local Government (the "Authority"), the buyer of consultancy services [para 1]. Fujitsu Services Ltd was initially an intermediary but dropped out and is not relevant [para 1].

### Amounts
- **Invoiced total / claimed:** £760,920 inclusive of VAT, across the invoices of 9 May 2011 and 25 October 2011 [para 3]. The split between the invoices and the net-of-VAT figure are not stated.
- **Paid against these invoices:** nil (the invoices are unpaid) [para 3].
- **Counterclaim:** none.
- **Interest claimed:** statutory interest under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 [para 5, Issue 7]; rate and amount not stated.

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **No minimum obligation:** "based on project requirement" in cl 2.1 meant there was no obligation to buy 500 days if the project did not need them, including after the closure of the Government Offices for the Regions (GOR), which was announced in July/October 2010 and took effect at the end of March 2011. Alternatively, estoppel or variation arising from the Claimant invoicing as it went [paras 5, 8, 15].
2. **No pro-rating for the final half-year** (1 April to 30 September 2011): no minimum applied to that period [para 16].
3. **Damages, not debt:** the obligation arose only on request; a failure to request gives only a damages claim [para 20].
4. **Availability not proved:** Claimant put to proof that it kept services available from 1 April 2009 to 30 September 2011, despite the GOR closure and the suspension email of 15 April 2011 [paras 5, 20].
5. **Penalty:** the minimum-payment clauses are an unenforceable penalty [para 24].
6. **VAT:** the £850 daily rate is VAT-inclusive, so VAT cannot be added [para 27].
7. **No qualifying debt / no valid invoice:** because the invoices wrongly added VAT, they were not "valid" invoices under cl 2.4 and statutory interest did not run [paras 33-34].
8. **Remission of interest under s.5 of the 1998 Act** for alleged delay in a two-month period in early 2012 before proceedings were issued on 30 May 2012 [para 38].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected.** "Based on project requirement" explains how the minimum was arrived at; it is not a condition. The Defendant's reading deprives "minimum" of meaning. Estoppel or variation is unarguable because there was no consideration, reliance or detriment [paras 14-15].
2. **Rejected.** The minimum is pro-rated to at least 250 days for the final six months [para 19].
3. **Rejected.** The obligation did not depend on a request, so the claim lies in debt; the Defendant's cross-application was dismissed [paras 21-23].
4. **Rejected.** Unchallenged evidence and contemporaneous documents (the 17 December 2010 minutes and the 15 April 2011 email) show the Claimant kept services available to the end of the term. Neither party terminated, so White & Carter v McGregor applies [paras 21-22].
5. **Rejected.** Even if a take-or-pay clause can in principle be a penalty, this one was commercially justified, freely negotiated at or below market rates and not in terrorem [paras 25-26].
6. **Upheld.** There was no express or implied agreement that VAT be added. Clause 3.7 and the contrast with the express VAT wording in the other agreements were decisive. Subsequent conduct cannot be used to construe the contract, and no rectification was pleaded [para 32].
7. **Rejected.** Following Ruttle Plant Hire, an invoice that overstates the sum (here by adding VAT) still gives notice of the amount. The invoice was valid but included a sum not due [paras 35-37].
8. **Rejected.** No relevant delay was shown, and without prejudice negotiations continued until April 2012; no reduction under s.5(4) [para 38].

### Money result
- Summary judgment for the Claimant for the sum claimed **save for VAT**; summary judgment for the Defendant dismissing the VAT element [para 39]. The resulting net figure is not stated in the judgment.
- Statutory interest under the 1998 Act runs on the debt with no remission [paras 37-38]; the interest computation and amount are not stated.
- Costs: not stated. The amendment to the Defence was unopposed "save as to costs" [para 3].

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* The case turned on the contract wording, one pre-contract email (15 May 2009) and two contemporaneous DCLG documents showing that services were still wanted or only "suspended". The debtor called no witness with first-hand knowledge (Mr Bentley had left), which left its construction arguments unsupported [paras 7, 21].
- *Commentary:* Lesson for collections: under a minimum-commitment contract, the creditor can invoice the unused balance as a debt if it can show it stayed ready, willing and able to perform and the contract was never terminated. Keep evidence of availability (meeting minutes, "suspension" emails).
- *Commentary:* The VAT point is a warning for Reconciliation. Where the contract is silent on VAT and does not say "plus VAT", Reconciliation should flag the risk that the price is VAT-inclusive, especially when the parties' other contracts deal with VAT expressly. Here the creditor lost the whole VAT element.
- *Commentary:* Challenge should reject the "invalid invoice, so no interest" argument: an overstated invoice still gives notice of the debt under s.4(5) of the 1998 Act (Ruttle), unless the contract expressly makes a correct invoice a condition of payment. Challenge should also reject remission arguments based on short pre-action gaps while negotiations were continuing.
- *Commentary:* Resolution: the right route is a court-claim pack for the net (ex-VAT) principal plus 1998 Act statutory interest. Because the judgment gives no final figure, Reconciliation would need to recalculate the principal net of VAT and the interest on it.
