# RAM Media Ltd (in administration) v The Ministry of Culture of the Hellenic Republic (Secretariat General of Sport) — [2008] EWHC 1835 (QB)
- **Court / venue:** High Court of Justice, Queen's Bench Division, Royal Courts of Justice, Strand, London (Case No TLQ/07/0461)
- **Judge:** The Hon. Mr Justice Tugendhat
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Hearing 5, 6, 9, 10, 12, 13, 17 to 20, 23 to 25 June and 15 to 18 July 2008; judgment dated 31 July 2008
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/qb/2008/1835
- **Tier:** borderline — an unpaid contractual instalment (€1.2m due 15 October 2006) was recovered as a debt after the payer tried to make payment conditional on performance and to renegotiate. Caveat: this is an event-hosting fee, not a supply of goods or services invoiced on delivery. The case is mainly about repudiation, an alleged oral variation and a wrongful call on a bank guarantee, and interest is not addressed.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Claimant / creditor:** RAM Media Ltd (in administration from 4 May 2007), exclusive licensee from FIFPro of the commercial rights in the 2006 FIFPro World XI Player Awards; organiser of the Awards with Parallel Media Group PLC (PMG) [paras 1-2, 6].
- **Defendant / debtor:** The Ministry of Culture of the Hellenic Republic (Secretariat General of Sport), host of the Awards in Athens, liable to pay a €4 million hosting fee [paras 1, 10].

### Amounts
- **Contract price:** €4,000,000 hosting fee in four instalments [para 10].
- **Paid:** first and second instalments, €2.4m in total; the Ministry then recovered €1.2m by calling the bank guarantee [paras 31, 208, 212, 241].
- **Claimed:** €2.8m unpaid (third instalment €1.2m, guarantee proceeds €1.2m, fourth instalment €400,000) less savings; lost sponsorship £75,000; Schedule of Loss also claimed £25,000, £3,319, US$11,000 and administration costs [paras 14-15, 245-246].
- **Savings conceded by RAM:** at least £278,051 (as pleaded); later put at €544,009 (Schedule of Loss, 2 May 2008) and €499,021 (Mr Manley's second statement) [paras 14-15, 240, 250]. The Ministry argued the savings were €4,072,663 [para 252].
- **Counterclaim:** declaration that the Ministry was entitled to call the €1.2m guarantee, and restitution of the €1.2m second instalment; a wasted expenditure claim was withdrawn [paras 20, 22].
- **Interest claimed:** not stated.

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **Oral variation on 27 October 2006** (fee reduced to €2.2m, deliverables transferred to the Ministry, Artist dropped), which RAM then repudiated; an earlier alleged agreement of 19 October was also relied on [paras 18-19, 117].
2. **Condition precedent:** payment of the 15 October instalment was conditional on RAM having performed its obligations due by then; 29 (later 37) alleged failures listed [paras 16-17, 170, 184].
3. **Termination only via Cl V 2 notice:** non-payment was remediable, so RAM had to serve a 30-day notice before terminating [para 21].
4. **Cl IV 2 repayment:** on cancellation, RAM must repay all instalments regardless of cause [paras 20, 216].
5. **Savings:** the claim must be reduced by RAM's savings, said to extinguish it (€4,072,663) [para 252].
6. **Late performance by RAM** (late information on winners, Artist) hampered the Ministry's sponsorship and ticket sales [paras 192-195].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected.** On 19 October there was a genuine misunderstanding about the terms, so no binding agreement [para 117]. On 27 October no agreement was ever reached on payment and negotiations simply broke down [para 165]. The Agreement remained binding until 31 October [para 169].
2. **Rejected.** The contention "has no merit". The staged payments were not tied to progress, and winners were not even notified by FIFPro until 16 October [paras 171-175].
3. **Rejected.** The Agreement did not restrict termination to Cl V, and a 30-day notice would have been futile given the Ministry's stance [para 210]. Refusing to pay except under a new contract was an actual breach as to the third instalment and an anticipatory breach as to the fourth, amounting to repudiation [paras 207-209].
4. **Rejected.** Both limbs of Cl IV 2 apply only to cancellation beyond the parties' reasonable control; the Ministry's reading would be "most unjust" [paras 217-218].
5. **Rejected as to the debt; relevant only to damages.** Savings cannot be deducted from a debt or from the account of the guarantee proceeds (White & Carter). RAM's pleaded concession was limited to damages, and the Ministry bore and failed to discharge the burden of proving greater savings [paras 244, 259, 266]. Savings did wipe out the damages claim for the fourth instalment [para 240].
6. **Not decided / no legal consequence.** Without a valid condition precedent, alleged breaches by RAM before 15 October had no legal effect; the judge made no findings on the heads [paras 175, 184].

### Money result
- Judgment for RAM for **€2.4 million** [para 269], made up of:
  - €1.2m third instalment (a debt, unconditionally accrued on 15 October 2006) [para 239]; and
  - €1.2m, for which the Ministry must account after wrongly calling the bank guarantee [paras 241-243].
- The fourth instalment (€400,000) sounds in damages and is extinguished by savings above €400,000 [para 240].
- Lost sponsorship (£75,000, about €111,000) is reduced by savings to about €12,000 in damages [paras 245, 259]. Whether that sum is included in the final order is not stated; the conclusion states only €2.4m.
- Unpleaded Schedule of Loss items and administration costs were rejected (not pleaded, and too remote) [para 247].
- Counterclaim: failed on the findings (no separate order stated).
- Interest and costs: not stated.

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* What decided the case was the plain wording of the payment schedule (fixed dates, "free and clear of any set-offs") together with the absence of any concluded variation. The Ministry's own drafts of 30 and 31 October contradicted its pleaded variation [paras 163-165].
- *Commentary:* Lesson for collections: an instalment that has fallen due on a fixed date is an accrued debt that survives termination, and the debtor cannot reduce it by the creditor's "savings". Collect and document each instalment promptly when due, and do not treat ongoing renegotiation as agreed until it is signed.
- *Commentary:* Challenge should reject a "condition precedent" defence unless the contract expressly links payment to performance milestones. It should also reject "you had to serve a cure notice first" where the debtor has clearly refused to pay except on new terms. Challenge should, however, separate accrued instalments (debt) from future instalments (damages, subject to mitigation and savings).
- *Commentary:* Reconciliation should treat money taken through a wrongful call on a performance guarantee as a sum to be accounted for to the creditor, and treat future instalments as damages net of costs saved.
- *Commentary:* Resolution: a court-claim pack for the accrued instalment plus the guarantee proceeds. Because the contract is governed by English law, interest would need to be claimed under s.35A Senior Courts Act 1981 or the 1998 Act; the judgment does not deal with interest.
