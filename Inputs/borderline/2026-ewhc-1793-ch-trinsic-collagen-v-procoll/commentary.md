# Trinsic Collagen Ltd v Procoll Ltd — [2026] EWHC 1793 (Ch)
- **Court / venue:** High Court of Justice, Chancery Division, Royal Courts of Justice, Rolls Building, Fetter Lane, London EC4A 1NL (Case No BL-2024-001317)
- **Judge:** Andrew de Mestre KC, sitting as a Deputy Judge of the High Court
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Hearing 21-24 and 27 April 2026; judgment Friday 17 July 2026
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/ch/2026/1793
- **Tier:** borderline; the buyer is the claimant, suing for over GBP 7m lost profits on allegedly defective collagen. The invoice-debt element arises only as the supplier's set-off: two unpaid batch invoices (GBP 15,000 and GBP 30,000) and a GBP 845,000 minimum-purchase claim. Useful for "no invoice/no delivery, no debt" and no-set-off clauses, but most of the judgment is about product testing, UCTA and lost profits.

### Amounts
- Debtor's claims: lost profits of GBP 7,181,538 (expert NPV) [paras 3, 245]; alternatively restitution of GBP 172,200 as pleaded (later GBP 149,940) [para 258]; Late Delivery Fees for the March, October and November Orders [para 251].
- Creditor's set-offs: GBP 845,000 for unpurchased Minimum Purchase Amounts from December 2023, rising monthly [para 271]; GBP 15,000 (October balance) and GBP 30,000 (November) plus contractual interest [para 279].
- Interest: contractual interest claimed on the unpaid invoices; rate not stated. No Late Payment Act or s.35A claim stated.

### Debtor's defences and complaints
(These are the buyer's answers to the supplier's set-off, together with its own complaints.)
1. **March Order out of specification** (TACC 6,900 cfu/g, result received 6 Apr 2023; never disclosed to Trinsic until 30 Jul 2024); no replacement batch supplied [paras 32, 40].
2. **Invalid/ambiguous Product Testing** (">300 cfu/g") for the April, August, September and October Orders [paras 42, 54-55, 61].
3. **Unfit for purpose: reformation** of the March to September Orders, first flagged 13 Jul 2023 (one container) and generally on 4 Oct 2023, months after collection [paras 50, 56].
4. **October Order**: only 2.4 kg taken; balance not payable because the batch never passed Product Testing [paras 57, 280].
5. **Minimum Purchase Amount (GBP 845,000)**: not payable because no Product was delivered or invoiced from December 2023 [paras 272-277].
6. **Clause 17 cap and loss-of-profit exclusion** said to be unreasonable under UCTA or disapplied for deliberate default [paras 4, 216].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Upheld.** Clause 8.11 was engaged and Procoll failed to supply a replacement batch, so Trinsic was entitled to a Late Delivery Fee capped at GBP 3,000 (10% of GBP 30,000) [paras 253-256, 282.1].
2. **Upheld as breach, but no money.** Clauses 8.8/8.9 were breached for the ambiguous results [para 282.3]. There was no deliberate default, so lost profits were barred by cl 17.7.2 [para 282.4].
3. **Upheld as breach, but no money.** The reformed parts of the March to September Orders were unfit for purpose, and Trinsic was not shown to have mishandled the product [paras 201, 212, 282.5]. Clause 17 was reasonable under UCTA, so lost profits were excluded [para 282.6]. Restitution failed because the Agreement had not been discharged and there was no total failure of basis, since much of the product was used [paras 265-269, 282.7].
4. **Upheld.** Payment is triggered by "Delivery", which requires Product that has passed Product Testing. The October result was ambiguous, so the GBP 15,000 balance was not recoverable [para 280].
5. **Upheld.** Clause 9.8 does not create a liability to pay without Delivery and an invoice. No invoices were rendered and no delay was declared, so nothing became due under cl 14.2 [paras 272-278].
6. **Rejected.** No deliberate default; clauses 17.5, 17.7 and 17.8 were reasonable [paras 282.4, 282.6].
- **November invoice: debtor had no defence.** That batch passed Product Testing (albeit after collection), so GBP 30,000 was payable without set-off under cl 14.5 [para 281].

### Money result
- Trinsic is entitled to GBP 3,000 (Late Delivery Fee, March Order) [para 282.1].
- Procoll's set-off of GBP 30,000 (unpaid November invoice) exceeds that sum, so no sum is due from Procoll to Trinsic [paras 281, 282.8, 283].
- Not stated: whether Procoll obtained judgment for the GBP 27,000 net balance, whether contractual interest was awarded, and costs.

## Commentary
*(Commentary: author's analysis, not the court's.)*
- The debt question turned on the contract's payment trigger. Payment was due 30 days after "Delivery", and Delivery was defined as making available Product that had passed testing. The invoice that had a clean test certificate (November) was recovered in full despite the buyer's quality complaints. The invoice for an ambiguously tested batch (October balance) and the uninvoiced minimum-purchase months (GBP 845,000) were not recovered.
- Lesson for collections teams: a take-or-pay or minimum-purchase entitlement has to be kept alive by actually making goods available and issuing invoices on the delivery dates. A creditor that stops supplying and invoicing cannot later claim the minimums as a lump sum.
- A no-set-off clause (cl 14.5) protects a properly triggered invoice: the November invoice was payable even though the buyer had a damages claim. But the clause cannot create a debt that never became due.
- DisputeOS mapping: Reconciliation should mark each batch as due or not due according to whether a passed test certificate exists (the "proof of performance" document), and should not compute minimum-purchase sums for months with no invoice. Challenge should uphold the buyer on the October balance and the GBP 845,000. It should reject withholding on the November invoice (passed test, cl 14.5), and should treat late quality complaints (13 Jul and 4 Oct 2023, months after collection) as a damages issue capped by cl 17 rather than a defence to the debt. Resolution: a letter before action or court-claim pack for GBP 30,000 with contractual interest, net of any conceded GBP 3,000 Late Delivery Fee.
