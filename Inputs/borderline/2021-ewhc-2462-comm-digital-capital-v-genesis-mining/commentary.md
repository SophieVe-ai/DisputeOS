# Digital Capital Ltd v Genesis Mining Iceland ehf — [2021] EWHC 2462 (Comm)
- **Court / venue:** High Court of Justice, Business and Property Courts of England and Wales, Queen's Bench Division, Commercial Court, The Rolls Building, 7 Rolls Buildings, Fetter Lane, London EC4A 1NL (Case No CL-2019-000606)
- **Judge:** Miss Julia Dias QC, sitting as a Deputy High Court Judge
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Hearing 12-15 and 19-22 July 2021; judgment handed down remotely 8 September 2021
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/comm/2021/2462
- **Tier:** borderline; a service provider recovers GBP 2,484,046 of unpaid monthly invoices after the client stopped paying and then purported to terminate for alleged non-performance. Caveat: this is a split trial on liability only; the services are bespoke software and regulatory work rather than goods; and the outcome turns mainly on repudiation law rather than invoice evidence.

- **Creditor (Claimant):** Digital Capital Ltd, an FCA-authorised e-money institution providing regulatory set-up, core banking software and operational services; represented at trial by its director Mrs Arlov in person [paras 3, 12, 14, 18].
- **Debtor (Defendant):** Genesis Mining Iceland ehf, the client building the "Genesis Wallet" crypto/fiat product [paras 6-7, 17].

### Amounts
- Claimed and unpaid invoices: GBP 2,484,046 [para 214].
- Paid: GBP 800,000 + GBP 800,000, plus monthly invoices up to late 2018 (total of monthly payments not stated) [paras 60-61].
- Creditor's further claim: damages for wrongful repudiation (lost profits over the unexpired term), adjourned to a second trial [paras 2, 216].
- Debtor's counterclaim: wasted expenditure/lost profits, with restitution of earlier payments abandoned at trial [paras 2, 160].
- Interest: contractual. Claimed at 3.75% p.a. daily, compounding quarterly; the judge assumed this was under cl 19 (3% over RBS base rate from due date) [para 215].

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **Implied term** that operational/maintenance fees were not chargeable if Go Live (10 Jul 2018) was missed or development stalled [para 162].
2. **Repudiatory breach by failure to deliver a functioning system** within a reasonable time. Raised formally only in the letter of 3 Jun 2019, after invoices had gone unpaid since late 2018; no cl 16.1 notice [paras 148, 164-176].
3. **Repudiatory failure to provide financial information / proof of development** (request of 27 Nov 2018 tied to a potential outside investment) [paras 207-211].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected.** Clause 30.2 excluded all implied terms, and counsel did not resist that conclusion [para 163].
2. **Rejected.** Clause 16.5 preserved a common-law right to terminate [paras 174-175]. Some "category i" functionalities were missing and were material breaches [paras 203-204]. But they were not repudiatory: the system worked; the gaps could be remedied, many within 30 days; Genesis never supplied the specifications despite 11 requests; and it made no complaint on 24 Mar 2019 [para 205]. Genesis's termination was itself a repudiatory breach [para 212]. The judge also found that Genesis stopped paying for financial reasons, not because of complaints about performance [para 105].
3. **Rejected.** Information about shareholdings was contractually irrelevant, and Digital Capital was not unwilling to show the state of its system [paras 208-211].

### Money result
- Judgment for Digital Capital on its outstanding invoices of GBP 2,484,046 [para 214].
- Interest and costs were left for further submissions; the rate (3.75% compounded as claimed, or cl 19 at 3% over RBS base) was not decided [para 215].
- Damages for Genesis's repudiation are to be assessed at a second trial [para 216]. Genesis's counterclaim fails on liability, since it was not entitled to terminate [paras 212-213].

## Commentary
*(Commentary: author's analysis, not the court's.)*
- What decided the case was the timing and contemporaneous record. The client stopped paying for its own financial reasons (19 Nov 2018 message), asked for time to pay on 24 Mar 2019 without raising any performance complaint, and only alleged repudiatory breach in a solicitor's letter on 3 Jun 2019, shortly before the agreed payment deadline. The judge saw that letter as a likely "pre-emptive strike" [paras 156-157].
- Even proven material breaches did not defeat the invoices. The fees were payable under the schedule irrespective of Go Live, implied terms were excluded, and the client never used the 30-day notice-to-remedy mechanism. Collections teams should check whether the debtor followed the contract's own breach and cure process before treating a performance complaint as a defence.
- The creditor's file of repeated written requests for the client's specifications (cl 4.2 assistance) was powerful evidence that delay was not its fault.
- DisputeOS mapping: Evidence should line up the debtor's payment history against its first written complaint. Challenge should treat a complaint first raised months after payments stopped, and after a request for time to pay, as weak, and should reject an "implied no-fee-before-Go-Live" defence where an entire-agreement/exclusion clause exists. Reconciliation should compute contractual interest under cl 19 (3% over base) from each invoice due date, noting the claimed 3.75% compounding basis was unresolved. Resolution: a court-claim pack for the GBP 2,484,046 debt, with lost-profit damages kept as a separate later track.
