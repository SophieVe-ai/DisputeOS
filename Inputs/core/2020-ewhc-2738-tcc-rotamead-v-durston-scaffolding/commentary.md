# Rotamead Ltd v Durston Scaffolding Ltd, Jamie Ryan and Paul Durston — [2020] EWHC 2738 (TCC)
- **Court / venue:** High Court, Technology and Construction Court, Rolls Building, Fetter Lane, London EC4A 1NL (judgment given via Skype)
- **Judge:** Martin Bowdery QC (sitting as a Deputy Judge of the High Court)
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Judgment delivered Thursday 27 August 2020; terms of order agreed and signed 30 September 2020 [para 61]
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/tcc/2020/2738
- **Tier:** core: scaffolding hire charges and a novated historic debt claimed from a hirer and two personal guarantors, defended by economic duress, a "trilateral agreement" shifting liability, an overpayment reconciliation and a VAT invoice point; won on the creditor's unchallenged document trail.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Creditor / claimant:** Rotamead Ltd, supplier (hirer-out) of scaffolding equipment [para 2].
- **Debtor / first defendant:** Durston Scaffolding Ltd ("DSL"), scaffolding contractor incorporated 17 February 2017 to take over the business of Paul Durston Ltd ("PDL"), which entered creditors' voluntary liquidation on 24 May 2017 [paras 2 to 4, chronology].
- **Second defendant:** Jamie Ryan, director of DSL, personal guarantor from 15 June 2017 [paras 5, 29].
- **Third defendant:** Paul Durston, personal guarantor of DSL from 28 March 2017; represented himself and DSL at trial [paras 1, 4, 17].

### Amounts
- **Novated historic debt:** £186,564.48 at 31 March 2017 [para 53].
- **Invoiced 31 March to 7 August 2017:** about £71,221.21 [para 53].
- **Claimed in Particulars of Claim (issued 29 October 2018):** £226,711.21 plus accruing hire at £247.14 per day, delivery up of equipment or damages [paras 2, 55].
- **Balance at trial:** hire due to 21 August 2020 £386,423.77 gross (£322,019.81 + £64,403.96 VAT), after credits [paras 56, 57].
- **Equipment still on hire:** 215,962 ft galvanised tube, 16,584 double couplers, 21,003 single couplers, value £217,691.22 (£181,409.35 + VAT) [paras 55 to 57].
- **Counterclaim:** DSL counterclaim for overpaid hire, alleged overpayment £17,123.90 (invoiced £297,839.28 v paid £314,963.18) [paras 6, 7, 34].
- **Interest claimed:** contractual rate under cl 3.4 (higher of 1.75% over Barclays base or the 1998 Act rate); £65,816.92 at judgment [paras 29, 57]. Claimant also beat its own Part 36 offer (CPR 36.17(4)) [para 60].

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **Economic duress:** novation (31 March 2017) and Mr Ryan's guarantee (15 June 2017) procured by threats to enter sites and remove scaffolding [paras 6, 34, 35].
2. **Construction of guarantee:** Mr Ryan not liable for the historic debt or DSL debts before 15 June 2017; contra proferentem [paras 41, 42].
3. **"Trilateral agreement" of 18 April 2017:** Higgins accepted 469,028 ft of materials on its sites and sole liability for shortages, so DSL wrongly invoiced; raised first in closing and not pleaded [paras 21 to 23, 34, 46].
4. **Overpayment / counterclaim:** reconciliation showing DSL overpaid by £17,123.90; first raised about May 2018 [paras 7, 34, 50].
5. **Authenticity of three documents** challenged in cross-examination without notice [para 8].
6. **VAT invoice point:** no VAT invoices issued to DSL for the historic balance; Rotamead should have written off PDL's invoices and reissued [para 58].
7. **Rotamead wrote off the historic debt** in its accounts (Mr Winston's evidence; unpleaded) [para 8].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected:** no bullying or illegitimate pressure on the facts; refusal to contract or a bona fide claim to repossess is not duress (Times Travel v PIA); in any event the novation would be voidable only and DSL affirmed it [paras 16, 17, 20, 30, 32, 36 to 40].
2. **Rejected:** the guarantee covers DSL's obligations under the General Terms, which by novation included the historic debt; the 16 June 2017 letter shows the parties so understood it [paras 41 to 44].
3. **Rejected:** unpleaded, unsupported by Mr Durston's evidence, contrary to the contemporaneous emails, and Mr Ryan himself said DSL was not party to it [paras 22 to 27, 47 to 49].
4. **Rejected:** Rotamead's six lever arch files of records were unchallenged; the defendants' 24 May 2017 schedule was inaccurate; Mr Durston abandoned the counterclaim [paras 51 to 53].
5. **Rejected:** no notice under CPR 32.19, so authenticity deemed admitted [para 8].
6. **Rejected:** novation transferred the obligation to pay; no new supply and no need for write-off or new VAT invoices [paras 58, 59].
7. **Rejected:** speculation, unpleaded [para 8].

### Money result
Order (terms agreed by counsel) [para 60]:
- Judgment for Rotamead; DSL's counterclaim dismissed; Ryan and Durston declared liable to indemnify Rotamead.
- Defendants to pay **£492,514.19**: £387,906.61 unpaid hire (£323,255.51 + VAT), £65,816.92 contractual interest, and £45,372.35 under CPR 36.17(4)(d).
- Delivery up of the equipment by 4 pm 24 September 2020, with £247.14 per day (£205.95 + VAT) until returned; failing delivery, £230,889.60 (£217,691.22 plus £13,198.38 under CPR 36.17(4)(d)).
- Costs of claim and counterclaim on the indemnity basis, with interest at 10% from 20 November 2018; £120,000 on account.
- Note: the hire figure in the order (£387,906.61) differs from the £386,423.77 computed at para 56; the judgment does not explain the difference.

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* The decisive evidence was the creditor's complete, contemporaneous paper trail (hire notes, off-hire checklists, statements of account), which the defendants never reviewed. A debtor's desktop reconciliation cannot beat unchallenged primary records.
- *Commentary:* Collections lesson: when restructuring a failing customer's debt, take a written novation from both companies, a fresh guarantee tied to the payment-plan concession, and keep the concession letter. Each of these defeated a defence here.
- *Commentary:* Challenge should reject duress arguments where the creditor merely insisted on payment or return of its own goods in good faith, and should treat late, unpleaded liability-shifting stories (the "trilateral agreement") as weak when contemporaneous emails contradict them.
- *Commentary:* Reconciliation should rebuild the opening balance from the novated sum plus invoices less payments (as the court did, within £662.50) and credit third-party hire payments; it should run ongoing hire per day until return.
- *Commentary:* Resolution: an early Part 36 offer paid off here (enhanced sums and indemnity costs under CPR 36.17(4)). A court-claim pack should combine debt, contractual interest, guarantor liability and delivery up with a value-in-lieu alternative.
