# International Media Advertising Ltd v Ministry of Culture and Tourism of the Republic of Turkey (Sohan Singh, Third Party) — [2018] EWHC 3285 (QB)

- **Court / venue:** High Court, Queen's Bench Division, Royal Courts of Justice, Strand, London WC2A 2LL
- **Judge:** David Edwards QC, sitting as a Deputy High Court Judge
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Heard 10 to 13 July and 20 September 2018; judgment dated Friday 7 December 2018
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/qb/2018/3285
- **Tier:** core: media buyer sues for 33 unpaid invoices; the customer proved the contract allowed only pass-through of actual media costs and that supplier invoices had been altered. The creditor recovered only four proven invoices and lost a larger deceit and breach counterclaim. A clear example of the creditor at fault.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Creditor / claimant:** International Media Advertising Ltd (IMA), small English media-buying company, the "Contracted Media Planning and Buying Partner" [paras 1, 64].
- **Debtor / defendant (counterclaimant):** Ministry of Culture and Tourism of the Republic of Turkey, acting through the Culture and Tourism Office at the Turkish Embassy in London (client) [para 2].
- **Third party:** Mr Sohan Singh, IMA's principal shareholder and sole director [para 1].
- **Other contracting party:** DDF International Ltd, the "Agency", paid a 5% commission on net media spend [paras 4, 24].

### Amounts
- Claimed: £250,700.02 and €170,450.00 (33 unpaid invoices), in debt or damages, alternatively quantum meruit / unjust enrichment [paras 3, 4, 246].
- Counterclaim pleaded: £911,460.73 overpaid over three campaigns (plus €17,526.32) and £45,573.00 excess DDF commission [paras 23, 24]; reduced at closing to £572,766.54 and €15,861.62 plus commission of £28,638.33 and €7,930.08 [para 28].
- Interest: not stated (left to consequential hearing) [para 305].

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **Contract allows only pass-through of actual media cost**, supported by genuine supplier invoices; IMA cannot charge a margin [paras 5, 7]. Complaint first raised over taxi advertising on 24 February 2012 [para 31].
2. **Non-admission that IMA incurred and paid the invoiced sums** for the 33 unpaid invoices [para 5].
3. **Counterclaim in breach of contract:** overcharging supported by altered supplier invoices across the 2009, 2010 and 2011 Campaigns [paras 8, 12].
4. **Counterclaim in deceit:** (a) inflated 2011 media plan figures represented as supplier costs; (b) altered supplier invoices represented as genuine [paras 10, 11].
5. **Claims against Mr Singh personally:** inducing breach of contract and deceit [para 13].
- IMA's answers: contract permitted it to "bill to the media plan" and keep any margin, or was varied by an understanding with the London Office; alterations were requested by the Ministry; waiver [paras 15 to 18, 253].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Upheld**: on construction IMA could bill only what it was liable to pay the media providers, with unaltered supplier invoices; the alleged variation/understanding was rejected and waiver was not made out [paras 239, 251 to 253].
2. **Upheld (in large part)**: given IMA's admitted practice of overbilling, it had to prove supplier prices and payment, and produced almost nothing; only four invoices were proven by emails or genuine supplier invoices (£12,450.00 and €950.00). The unjust enrichment alternative failed because a subsisting contract fixed remuneration [paras 240 to 248].
3. **Upheld**: IMA in breach for altered invoices and overcharging [para 253].
4. **Upheld**: both deceit limbs made out: a reasonable client would read "net cost" as the supplier's figure without uplift; supplying altered copy invoices impliedly represented them as genuine; IMA knew and intended reliance [paras 262 to 270].
5. **Partly upheld**: Mr Singh not liable for inducing breach (director acting bona fide within authority: Said v Butt), but personally liable in deceit as the person who made the inflated figures and knew of the alterations [paras 274, 279 to 283].
- Quantum was assessed row by row by supplier and year on the imperfect Counterclaim Schedule, netting years where IMA undercharged and drawing inferences against IMA because it had destroyed records [paras 289, 294 to 300].

### Money result
- IMA recovers £12,450.00 and €950.00 on its claim, which otherwise fails [para 303].
- Ministry's loss: Overpaid Balance £349,772.55 and €14,274.12, plus 5% DDF commission £17,488.63 and €713.71 = £367,261.18 and €14,987.83; judgment against IMA (contract and deceit) and Mr Singh (deceit) [paras 300 to 304].
- Set-off of the two judgments is not stated (arithmetically the Ministry would be net about £354,811.18 and €14,037.83).
- Interest and costs left for consequential submissions [para 305].

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* What decided the case was the contract wording (net cost, certified supplier invoices, discounts passed on exactly) together with IMA's own admissions that supplier invoices were altered. Once the creditor's documents were shown to be unreliable, the burden of proving each unpaid invoice became one it could not meet, because it had not kept original supplier invoices or proof of payment.
- *Commentary:* Collections lesson: on cost-plus or pass-through contracts the creditor must hold the underlying supplier invoices and proof of payment for every rebilled line. Destroying them after a campaign is fatal, and in a fraud context the court will infer against the party that destroyed them.
- *Commentary:* For DisputeOS: Evidence should flag any supporting third-party invoice whose number matches an original but whose amount differs (the pattern used throughout the schedule). Reconciliation should compare rebilled amounts against supplier invoices per supplier per year, netting under-charges, as the judge did. Challenge should uphold "you may only pass through actual cost" where the contract says net cost and certified invoices, and treat the first written complaint (24 February 2012) as the start of the dispute.
- *Commentary:* Resolution should not issue a letter before action here; it should propose a credit note or refund covering overbilling beyond verified supplier cost, and limit any claim to the four invoices with independent price evidence.
