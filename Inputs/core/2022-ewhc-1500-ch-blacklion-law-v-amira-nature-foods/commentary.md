# Blacklion Law LLP v Amira Nature Foods Ltd and Karan Chanana — [2022] EWHC 1500 (Ch)
- **Court / venue:** High Court, Business and Property Courts of England and Wales, Business List (ChD), Rolls Building, Fetter Lane, London EC4A 1NL (Case No BL-2018-000862)
- **Judge:** HHJ Paul Matthews (sitting as a Judge of the High Court)
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Hearing 23 to 26 May 2022; handed down remotely 10:30 am, 20 June 2022
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/ch/2022/1500
- **Tier:** core: a supplier of professional services sues on two unpaid fee invoices (£300,000) met by a "fee was contingent on completion" defence and a "we already paid in shares" defence; also shows standard terms carrying contractual interest into a later engagement letter, and personal liability of the controlling director.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Creditor / claimant:** Blacklion Law LLP, small London law firm (managing partner Ms Negar Yazdani), supplier of legal and transaction management services [paras 28, 32].
- **Debtor / first defendant:** Amira Nature Foods Ltd, BVI rice distributor, then NYSE listed; client [para 29].
- **Second defendant:** Karan Chanana, chairman, majority shareholder and admitted de facto controller of the first defendant; sued for inducing breach of contract [paras 29, 94].

### Amounts
- **Invoiced (Avatar):** £300,000 (£30,000 + £270,000) [paras 76, 77, 105].
- **Paid:** nil in cash; shares issued but unrealisable [paras 85, 86].
- **Claimed:** £300,000 debt against the first defendant; equivalent damages against the second defendant; alternatively rectification [paras 1, 2].
- **Interest claimed:** contractual, 1.5% per month from 30 days after invoice under cl 35 of the Terms of Business [para 62].
- **Counterclaim:** served 16 April 2020, discontinued August 2020 [para 3].
- **Related (general retainer, not in issue at trial):** summary judgment 15 February 2021; assessed at £65,000 plus £83,000 interest (£148,000), plus £37,000 costs, unpaid at trial [para 4].

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **Fee contingent on completion by 31 May 2017:** the words "subject to the completion of the Matter by 31 May 2017" meant no completion, no fee; the bond issue fell through in June or July 2017. First raised in the defence (16 April 2020); never raised in correspondence before proceedings [paras 12, 48, 77].
2. **No contractual interest:** the Avatar Retainer does not provide for interest and the reference to the Terms of Business was deleted [paras 62, 74].
3. **Already paid in shares:** shares worth £300,000 were issued; any loss is the claimant's own fault for not obtaining another US lawyer's opinion [paras 85, 86].
4. **Invoices inflated / timesheets required:** queries in January and February 2018 after repeated timesheet requests from July 2017 [paras 76, 77].
5. **Second defendant lacked knowledge:** he genuinely believed the fee was contingent, so no knowing inducement of breach [para 97].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected:** the wording was ambiguous; business common sense favoured reading "subject to" as capping the fixed fee for work to 31 May, not making it contingent; no market practice of wholly contingent fees for New York bond lawyers. Alternatively, rectification would have been granted on the 24 and 25 April 2017 emails and the share issue as outward expressions of accord [paras 39, 55 to 61, 81 to 84].
2. **Rejected:** the Terms of Business applied to all services unless varied, and SRA requirements meant they could not be wholly excluded; cl 35 interest applies [paras 64, 65].
3. **Rejected:** an implied term required the company to do what was reasonably necessary to allow sale; the transfer agent required the issuer's counsel opinion, which the company withheld, so it was in breach; no credit for any residual share value [paras 87, 93, 105].
4. **Rejected (not pursued at trial):** timesheet accuracy was not challenged in cross-examination [para 41].
5. **Rejected:** he approved the drafts before the disputed wording existed, twice said the invoices were approved, and shifted his story; liable in tort for procuring the breach [paras 98 to 104].

### Money result
- First defendant to pay **£300,000** debt plus contractual interest at **1.5% per month** from 30 days after the date of each invoice; payable within 14 days of hand-down [para 105]. Interest total not calculated in the judgment.
- Second defendant to pay **£300,000 damages** for procuring the breach [para 105].
- No credit for the blocked shares [para 105].
- Costs: not stated. Form of order left to a minute of order, preferably agreed [para 105].

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* What decided the case was the contemporaneous email trail: the defendants' own adviser called it a "flat fee", the company issued shares "as compensation for services rendered", and nobody mentioned contingency in a year of chasing. A defence that first appears in pleadings two years later carries little weight against that.
- *Commentary:* Collections lesson: payment "in kind" (shares, vouchers, credits) is not payment if the creditor cannot realise it because of the debtor's own non-cooperation. Track whether non-cash settlement has actually been converted.
- *Commentary:* Challenge should test ambiguous conditional pricing language against the drafting history only where rectification is pleaded, and otherwise against commercial common sense; here it should reject the contingency defence because it was never raised in correspondence after the trigger date passed.
- *Commentary:* Reconciliation should treat the shares as unpaid and compute 1.5% per month from 30 days after 13 June / 4 July 2017 (£30,000) and 25 September 2017 (£270,000); which invoice date applies to the reissued £30,000 invoice is not stated, so both scenarios should be shown.
- *Commentary:* Resolution should check whether master terms of business carry interest and other rights into later engagement letters, and, where a controlling director personally blocks payment, consider naming the director in the court-claim pack.
