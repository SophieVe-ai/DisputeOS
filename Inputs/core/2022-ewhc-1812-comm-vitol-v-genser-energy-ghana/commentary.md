# Vitol SA v Genser Energy Ghana Ltd — [2022] EWHC 1812 (Comm)

- **Court / venue:** High Court, Business and Property Courts of England and Wales, London Circuit Commercial Court (QBD), Rolls Building, Fetter Lane, London
- **Judge:** Ms Lesley Anderson QC, sitting as a Deputy Judge of the High Court
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Heard 13, 14, 15 and 20 June 2022; judgment dated 22 July 2022
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/comm/2022/1812
- **Tier:** core: propane seller on 90-day credit terminates for a missed invoice due date and claims the unpaid balance of a contractual settlement amount, with an alternative unpaid-invoices claim; covers due-date construction, informal default notices, a quantity-dispute time bar, unagreed contractual interest and the 1998 Act's s.12 "significant connection" test.

### Parties
- **Creditor / claimant:** Vitol SA, Swiss energy and commodities trader (seller of propane) [para 1].
- **Debtor / defendant:** Genser Energy Ghana Ltd, Ghanaian power producer (buyer) [para 1].

### Amounts
- Settlement Amount demanded: USD 17,560,514.02 (includes USD 19,654.11 interest) [paras 69, 70].
- Principal claim: unpaid balance of Settlement Amount USD 3,582,365.95 [para 2].
- Alternative Unpaid Invoices Claim: USD 559,281.16, reduced by USD 91,000 after Vitol's FSU concessions [paras 2, 33.1].
- Genser's credit claims for overpayment: USD 26,017.65 (abandoned) [para 33.2].
- Interest claimed: contractual 8% over LIBOR (Seventh Addendum); alternatively Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 at 8% over base; alternatively s.35A Senior Courts Act 1981 [paras 124, 125, 144].

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **Invoice due date:** revised February invoice S1907772 not due until 9 June or 30 July 2019 (90 days from issue or re-issue), so no default on 30 May 2019 [para 84].
2. **Default Notice invalid in form:** the 30 May 2019 email was a routine chaser, did not mention the 2-day cure or termination consequences [paras 90, 91]. First raised on the eve of trial [para 103].
3. **Default Notice not validly served:** not sent to all designated addressees (Ms Menyah, generic FUEL address) [paras 96, 97].
4. **Time bar on June 2018 cargo invoice (USD 83,262.71):** quantity disputed within days of discharge (protest 23 June 2018; emails 3 and 17 July 2018); proceedings not issued within one year [paras 51, 108].
5. **No obligation to pay late payment interest** (Seventh Addendum never agreed) [paras 31.4, 114].
6. **Force majeure** from 27 June 2019 (NPA letter of 17 May 2019, received 21/22 May) excusing USD 52,000 of June 2019 FSU charges [paras 120 to 122].
7. **FSU unavailability** for 1 day in April and 6 days in May 2019 (Vitol's force majeure of 29 April 2019) [paras 31.2, 31.3].
8. **1998 Act inapplicable** under s.12 (no significant connection with England) [paras 126 to 128].
9. Abandoned before or at trial: invoice reconciliation convention, penalty, frustration, invalid termination service, short delivery, overcharging, Settlement Amount future costs, commercial unreasonableness [paras 27, 28, 70].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected**: "invoicing date" means the last business day of the delivery month, so February product was due 29 May 2019; in any event the parties expressly agreed on 27 March 2019 that revised invoices kept the original due dates [paras 86, 88].
2. **Rejected**: the clause only required "notice to the defaulting party to make the payment"; a demand for payment sufficed [para 95].
3. **Rejected**: strict compliance was not a condition precedent; the notice was also a "Financial" matter, and service on one addressee (the CEO and Financial Controller here) was enough [para 99]. Alternatively Genser was estopped by convention, having responded without objection and promised to pay the "Settlement Amount" [paras 102 to 105].
4. **Upheld**: the inspector's figure is determinative only unless challenged within 45 days; Genser did challenge, and Vitol's claim was extinguished by the one-year bar (claim form issued 18 February 2020) [paras 108, 111, 112].
5. **Upheld**: no general interest agreement; Genser agreed interest only on two specific occasions (13 and 27 March 2019) [paras 63, 115, 116].
6. **Rejected**: Vitol had already terminated on 6 June 2019; the NPA letter made performance costlier, not impossible; force majeure excludes accrued payment obligations [para 123].
7. **Conceded by Vitol**: USD 91,000 credit allowed [para 33.1].
8. **Rejected**: payment to Vitol's account in London and Vitol carrying on relevant business in London gave a significant connection; 1998 Act interest applies; otherwise s.35A interest at US prime plus 1% would have been awarded [paras 143, 144].

### Money result
- Vitol's Settlement Amount claim succeeds; Genser's challenges fail [para 106]. Claimed unpaid balance: USD 3,582,365.95 [para 2]. The judgment does not state whether the USD 19,654.11 interest component inside the Settlement Amount is to be removed in light of the finding on contractual interest; final judgment sum not stated.
- Alternative invoice claim (not needed): June 2018 invoice time-barred; interest invoice not payable; USD 52,000 FSU charge payable [paras 112, 116, 123].
- Interest: statutory interest under the 1998 Act (8% over Bank of England base rate) rather than the 8% over LIBOR contractual rate [paras 124, 125, 143].
- Form of order and consequential matters (including costs and interest calculation) left to the parties to agree, or to be dealt with at hand-down [para 145].

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* What decided the case was the contract's mechanics plus the debtor's own words: a clear "last business day plus 90 days" due date, an email agreeing revised invoices kept original due dates, and the debtor's prompt replies treating the default and termination as valid. Technical objections raised years later did not survive that record.
- *Commentary:* Two creditor-side failures cost money: Vitol never got the late-interest addendum signed (its own staff noticed the gap internally), and it let a disputed USD 83k cargo invoice run past a one-year contractual time bar. Collections teams should diary contractual time bars from the date of delivery, not from the end of negotiations.
- *Commentary:* For cross-border contracts with English law, 1998 Act interest is not automatic; Reconciliation should record the payment destination and where the creditor does business so s.12 can be assessed, and fall back to s.35A where needed.
- *Commentary:* For DisputeOS: Challenge should uphold a quantity dispute notified within the contractual window (here an inspector protest on the day of discharge) and then check whether the creditor sued within the limitation clause; it should reject late technical notice objections where the debtor acknowledged the default in writing. Reconciliation should apply interest only where a signed term or specific agreement exists, otherwise statutory interest. Resolution: court-claim pack for the Settlement Amount balance with 1998 Act interest; credit note for the conceded FSU downtime (USD 91,000) and write-off of the time-barred June 2018 balance.
