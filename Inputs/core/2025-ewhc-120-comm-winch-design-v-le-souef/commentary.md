# Winch Design Ltd v Carl Le Souef (Somnio Superyachts Pty Ltd, Part 20 Defendant) — [2025] EWHC 120 (Comm)

- **Court / venue:** High Court, King's Bench Division, Business and Property Courts, Commercial Court, Rolls Building, Fetter Lane, London EC4A 1NL
- **Judge:** Ms Lesley Anderson KC, sitting as a Deputy Judge of the High Court
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Heard 28 and 29 October 2024; handed down remotely 23 January 2025
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/comm/2025/120
- **Tier:** core: yacht designer sues for three unpaid stage invoices under a signed fee proposal; the debtor ran wrong-counterparty, "pay only for work done", oral forbearance and non-performance/counterclaim defences, all rejected.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Creditor / claimant:** Winch Design Ltd, English yacht design firm (supplier of design services) [para 2].
- **Debtor / defendant:** Carl Le Souef, Australian entrepreneur, named as "the Client" in the contract (buyer of services) [paras 3, 13.1].
- **Part 20 defendant:** Somnio Superyachts Pty Ltd, Australian SPV for the project, co-founded by Mr Le Souef (sole director) and Capt. Erik Bredhe; the defendants said Somnio was the true counterparty [paras 4, 10].

### Amounts
- Contract price: £5,285,000 (Stage 6 procurement TBC) [para 13.6].
- Invoiced under Addendum: £933,750; paid £200,000; claimed balance £733,750 (invoices 9455, 9485, 9502) [para 9].
- Counterclaim: unquantified damages (costs of alternative suppliers, delay costs, value of services not performed) [para 95].
- Interest claimed: contractual, under clause 5.6 at 3% per annum above Base Rate [para 100].

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **Contracting party issue:** Somnio, not Mr Le Souef, was the client, by construction, rectification or estoppel by convention; invoices were addressed to Somnio and Winch's ledgers named Somnio [paras 10, 66, 69].
2. **Payment terms issue:** invoices are payable only once the corresponding stage work is (substantially) performed, not as accrued debts on dates [paras 75 to 78]. First objection of this kind raised 17 November 2020 for earlier invoices [para 18].
3. **Forbearance issue:** oral promise at the 22 October 2021 dinner not to demand payment or sue until Somnio obtained third-party funding (collateral contract / promissory estoppel) [paras 31, 82 to 84].
4. **Winch performance issue:** Winch failed to provide services covered by the Disputed Invoices and was not entitled to suspend [paras 10, 95]. Complaint of poor performance first relied on in evidence; the judge found the Addendum was driven by the client's shipyard delays, not Winch's performance [para 24].
5. **Counterclaim** for damages for non-performance [para 95].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected**: the contract unambiguously names Mr Le Souef as Client and was signed on his behalf; no outward expression of accord that Somnio was the party, and invoicing Somnio merely continued pre-contract practice; no common assumption or detriment for estoppel [paras 67, 71 to 74].
2. **Rejected**: on the wording (payment schedule, cl 5.2, 5.6) the Disputed Invoices were accrued debts payable 30 days from invoice; acknowledgement-based estoppel would also have been available but for lack of detriment [paras 80, 81].
3. **Rejected**: Mr Beharrell's account preferred; no contemporaneous record of any promise and the alleged promise was inherently improbable while Winch was pressing for payment [paras 90 to 92].
4. **Rejected**: Winch was entitled to suspend under cl 12.1 after the 30 June and 16 August 2021 notices and validly terminated by its Reply of 16 November 2023; the timetable was a business estimate only; in any event Winch substantially performed [paras 96, 97, 99].
5. **Rejected**: fails on pleading, law and evidence: no proof of any loss, and any loss would be Somnio's as owner [paras 98, 99].

### Money result
- Declaration/order that Mr Le Souef is indebted to Winch in £733,750.00, with contractual interest under clause 5.6 at 3% per annum above Base Rate; counterclaim dismissed [para 100].
- Interest arithmetic, the form of order and consequential matters (including costs) were left to the parties to agree, failing which further submissions [para 101].

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* The decisive evidence was documentary: the signed Proposal naming the individual as Client, the Addendum reciting the same parties, and a string of the debtor's own emails acknowledging the debt and blaming funding. Invoices addressed to a related company did not shift liability away from the named contracting party.
- *Commentary:* Collections lesson: invoice the contracting party named in the contract. Addressing invoices to an SPV gave the debtor an arguable (though failed) defence and cost a trial. Also, record any meeting at which payment terms are discussed; Winch's same-week internal email defeated the oral forbearance story.
- *Commentary:* Contractual mechanics mattered: fixed-date instalments plus "30 days from invoice" made the invoices accrued debts regardless of stage progress, and the express suspension clause (15 days' written notice) protected Winch from a non-performance counterclaim once it served notice.
- *Commentary:* For DisputeOS: Reconciliation should compute £933,750 invoiced less £200,000 paid = £733,750, with interest per invoice from day 31 at 3% over base (contractual rate displaces the 1998 Act). Challenge should reject the counterparty defence where the contract names the debtor and flag the invoice addressee mismatch as a risk; it should rebut "pay only for work done" when payment terms are date-based, and reject an undocumented oral forbearance contradicted by later acknowledgement emails. Resolution: letter before action with contractual interest, then court-claim pack; no credit note is warranted.
