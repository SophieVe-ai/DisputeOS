# Ocean Healthcare Ltd v Sigma Pharmaceuticals plc — [2014] EWCA Civ 1468

- **Court / venue:** Court of Appeal (Civil Division), Royal Courts of Justice, Strand, London (on appeal from HHJ Birtles, Mayor's and City of London County Court)
- **Judge:** Gloster LJ (lead judgment), Kitchin LJ and Arden LJ agreeing
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Heard 22 July 2014; judgment dated 24 November 2014 (further written submissions from the appellant received 17 and 18 November 2014)
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2014/1468
- **Tier:** core: classic unpaid-invoice claim by a wholesaler against a pharmacy where the debtor denied ordering and delivery and alleged forged delivery notes; signed delivery notes, payment history and absence of contemporaneous complaint won the case.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Creditor / claimant (respondent on appeal):** Sigma Pharmaceuticals plc, manufacturer, marketer and wholesale distributor of pharmaceutical and ancillary products (supplier) [para 2].
- **Debtor / defendant (appellant):** Ocean Healthcare Ltd, a small independent pharmacy at 12 High Road, London E13 (buyer) [para 2].

### Amounts
- Total pleaded as owing at 15 November 2009 (after credit notes): approximately £47,679, including about £12,000 (pleaded as £12,724) said to be carried forward from 2008 [paras 3, 13].
- Summary judgment (Master Yoxall, 2010) for part of the claim; leave to defend limited to invoices totalling £21,376.31 [para 4] (elsewhere £21,376.71 plus VAT [paras 8, 19]; the judgment uses both figures).
- Missing delivery notes: invoices totalling £1,108.80 plus VAT [para 7].
- Judgment at trial: £25,997.49 inclusive of interest, plus costs [para 1].
- Interest basis: not stated.
- Counterclaim: none; the debtor alleged unspecified discounts not credited [para 3], quantum not stated.

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **No debt outstanding / all 2009 invoices paid; discounts not credited** (pleaded defence) [para 3]. No dated complaint stated.
2. **Goods never ordered** for the disputed 2008 invoices; no purchase orders or order confirmations disclosed; electronic order database "wiped" [paras 5, 13]. Raised in litigation; no complaint during 2008 despite receiving statements and paying [para 6].
3. **Goods never delivered; delivery notes forged**, and some delivery notes missing (£1,108.80) [paras 5, 7]. First raised in litigation, not contemporaneously.
4. **Procedural unfairness / no reminders**: no chasers for 2008 invoices; originals not made available; particulars of claim not amended; quantum discrepancies [paras 10, 13].
5. **Improper reliance on unsworn driver statements** and on Mr Robinson's first-time identification of signatories at trial [para 13].
6. **Fresh evidence**: handwriting expert report obtained after trial (28 November 2013) [paras 9, 14].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected**: the 2008 account was cleared by the £10,000 payment of 7 January 2009 and the claim concerned unpaid 2009 invoices for goods "undoubtedly received" [paras 18, 21]; no discount case is addressed in the appeal.
2. **Rejected**: invoices recorded specific order numbers; the debtor bore an evidential burden to show its own order records excluded the disputed orders and produced none [paras 22, 23].
3. **Rejected**: trial judge entitled to find delivery notes genuine and goods delivered and signed for; missing notes de minimis given the passage of time; evidential burden of proving forgery lay "fairly and squarely" on the debtor [paras 7, 8, 25].
4. **Rejected**: quantum clear after summary judgment; all invoices available at trial; no reason to chase 2008 invoices that had been paid [paras 19 to 21].
5. **Rejected**: the judge gave limited weight to unsworn statements but could accept them alongside the documents; the delivery notes had been in the debtor's possession for months and it could have asked its three staff [paras 24, 25].
6. **Rejected**: fails Ladd v Marshall: obtainable with reasonable diligence and would not have influenced the result given that signatures were "no more than squiggles" [para 26].

### Money result
- Appeal dismissed [para 27]; trial judgment of £25,997.49 inclusive of interest in favour of Sigma, with Sigma's costs of the action, stands [paras 1, 27].
- Interest rate and basis: not stated. Costs of the appeal: not stated. A stay of execution had been granted pending appeal [para 11].

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* The decisive evidence was the chain invoice (with order number) to driver delivery note (with package count and a pharmacy signature), corroborated by consistent driver evidence and by the debtor's own conduct: it received statements and paid the 2008 invoices without complaint [paras 6, 21, 22].
- *Commentary:* Silence is powerful. The absence of any contemporaneous complaint about non-delivery or unordered goods was treated as making the forgery case inherently improbable. Collections teams should keep account statements and record that they were sent.
- *Commentary:* Payment appropriation matters. The creditor was entitled to appropriate the undesignated £10,000 to the oldest balance, which answered the "you never chased the 2008 invoices" point. Reconciliation should apply unallocated payments oldest-first (unless the debtor designated them) and show the running account so it is clear which invoices remain open.
- *Commentary:* For DisputeOS: Challenge should test non-delivery and non-ordering defences against signed delivery notes and against whether any complaint was made before the claim; here it should rebut them. Unsigned or missing delivery notes for a small fraction of the value (here about 5%) should be flagged but not treated as fatal where the pattern of trade is established. Resolution would reasonably have moved to a court-claim pack, since the debtor's dispute appeared only after supply stopped.
- *Commentary:* The case also shows that a late forensic challenge (handwriting expert after trial) will not reopen a judgment. Evidence should be gathered before trial.
