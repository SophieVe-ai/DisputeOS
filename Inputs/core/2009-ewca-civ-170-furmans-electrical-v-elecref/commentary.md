# Furmans Electrical Contractors v Elecref Ltd — [2009] EWCA Civ 170
- **Court / venue:** Court of Appeal (Civil Division), Royal Courts of Justice, Strand, London WC2A 2LL, on appeal from Great Grimsby County Court (Mr Recorder Burrell QC)
- **Judge:** Waller LJ (Vice-President), Arden LJ and Moore-Bick LJ; leading judgment by Waller LJ
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Hearing 16 February 2009; judgment 10 March 2009
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2009/170
- **Tier:** core: a subcontractor's claim on six unpaid invoices met by an overcharging defence and a counterclaim to reopen invoices already paid; the Court of Appeal separates the two and holds that paid invoices cannot be reopened where the payer paid after voicing doubts.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Creditor / claimant (respondent on appeal):** Furmans Electrical Contractors, cabling installation subcontractor to Elecref [para 2].
- **Debtor / defendant (appellant):** Elecref Ltd, itself a subcontractor supplying electrical items on many contracts, which engaged Furmans first on a meterage basis and from about July 2004 (and mainly from December 2006) on a daily rate [paras 2, 20].

### Amounts
- **Invoiced total (unpaid invoices in suit):** £23,203.31 across six invoices [para 12].
- **Paid:** earlier invoices to September 2007 paid (late); £26,129.99 paid on 2 October 2007 [paras 4, 10]. Total of all paid invoices not stated.
- **Claimed balance:** £23,203.31 (proceedings issued 30 November 2007) [para 12].
- **Counterclaim:** £40,529.37 for overcharging on paid and unpaid invoices, on the basis of an alleged 11 or 12 hour day [para 14].
- **Interest claimed:** not stated.

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **Oral agreement that the daily rate was based on an 11 or 12 hour day** (said to be made around 28 July 2004), so Furmans' 9-hour days were overcharged. First raised in writing by fax of 5 September 2007, after many invoices had been checked and paid [paras 5, 14].
2. **Friday accommodation overcharge:** accommodation rate charged on Fridays when no accommodation was used (5 September 2007 fax) [paras 3, 5].
3. **Counterclaim to reopen paid invoices** (£40,529.37), based only on the alleged oral agreement; no mistake or misrepresentation pleaded [paras 14, 41].
4. **Furmans' cross-point (respondent's notice):** a course of dealing fixed 9 hours as the basis; the recorder's "reasonable rate" finding was unpleaded [para 28].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected** (as an agreement): no express agreement on hours was proved, and Furmans' own "8 to 6" fax was found not received. However, the contract was for the agreed daily rate on a reasonable number of hours, which the recorder would have assessed at 11, so the unpaid invoices fall to be reduced to a reasonable sum [paras 33, 34, 35].
2. **Partly upheld** within the reasonable-sum assessment of the unpaid invoices; the Court of Appeal did not rule separately on Fridays but accepted Elecref's deductions on the unpaid invoices of about £3,591.02, rounded to £3,500 [paras 34, 44, 45].
3. **Rejected:** no oral agreement or representation was established; supervisors had checked invoices since February 2007; and by paying £26,129.99 after the 5 September 2007 protest Elecref assumed the risk and waived any claim to recover the money [paras 40 to 43].
4. **Furmans' course-of-dealing argument rejected** (invoices never spelt out hours) [para 36]; its appeal **allowed** because the recorder wrongly reopened paid invoices and failed to distinguish paid from unpaid [paras 38, 46].

### Money result
- Recorder had deducted £15,252 from £23,203.31 and given judgment for £7,751.31 plus costs [paras 1, 26].
- Court of Appeal: Furmans' appeal allowed, Elecref's appeal dismissed. Deduction of £3,500 from the sum claimed, which implies judgment of about £19,703.31 (arithmetic not stated in the judgment), subject to written submissions from counsel if the calculation was wrong [paras 44 to 46].
- Interest: not stated. Costs of the appeal: not stated. Final form of order left open to written submissions.

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* The decisive evidence was the payment of £26,129.99 on 2 October 2007, after the debtor had written on 5 September 2007 identifying exactly the overcharge it later sued on. Paying with knowledge of the complaint waived any right to recover; the same point would bar a Challenge agent from "clawing back" paid invoices in DisputeOS.
- *Commentary:* Collections lesson: invoices that state the pricing basis (hours per day, accommodation) protect the creditor. Furmans lost about £3,500 on the open invoices because its invoices and rate fax never stated hours, leaving the court to imply a reasonable basis.
- *Commentary:* Challenge should treat a complaint about pricing raised before payment of the disputed invoices as live against unpaid invoices only, and reject attempts to reopen invoices that were checked, signed off and paid, especially those paid after the complaint.
- *Commentary:* Reconciliation should keep paid and unpaid invoices in separate ledgers and compute any reasonable-sum deduction only against the open balance (here £23,203.31 less roughly £3,500).
- *Commentary:* Resolution: a settlement offer around the open balance less the debtor's identified per-invoice deductions would likely have matched the Court of Appeal result and avoided two levels of costly litigation, which the court itself criticised as disproportionate [para 29].
