# Longulf Trading (UK) Ltd v Niyazi Onen Gida San. A.S. and Mr Niyazi Onen — [2019] EWHC 1573 (Comm)
- **Court / venue:** High Court of Justice, Queen's Bench Division, Commercial Court, Rolls Building, Fetter Lane, London EC4A 1NL (Case No LM-2018-000024)
- **Judge:** Christopher Hancock QC (sitting as a Judge of the High Court)
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Hearing 8 February 2019; judgment 21 June 2019
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/comm/2019/1573
- **Tier:** core: unpaid trade invoices (18 instalments, US$2.47m) enforced against guarantors after an undefended trial; the court tests every defence the debtor could have run and splits recoverable contractual interest by what the written demand actually covered.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Creditor / claimant:** Longulf Trading (UK) Ltd ("LGT"), procurement agent which bought seafood and resold it to Dardanel [paras 8, 17].
- **Principal debtor (not a party):** Dardanel Onentas Gida Sanayi A.S., Turkish buyer [paras 9, 17].
- **Defendants:** (1) Niyazi Onen Gida San. A.S. and (2) Mr Niyazi Onen, joint and several guarantors of Dardanel's "Obligations" [paras 1, 16]. Defendants were served in Turkey, instructed solicitors but filed no defence and did not appear [para 2].

### Amounts
- **Invoiced / overdue principal:** US$2,472,615 [para 19].
- **Paid:** US$172,445 after the demand [para 32].
- **Claimed balance (principal):** US$2,300,170 [para 33].
- **Procurement fees claimed:** US$234,841.65 (1 June 2018 to 2 January 2019) [para 36].
- **Legal and other expenses claimed:** US$17,432.34 (Turkish counsel) and £16,161.85 (K&L Gates) [para 53].
- **Interest claimed:** contractual Default Rate of 15% p.a. under cl 3.9 / 8.1 (Schedule 4); figure left blank "[ ]" in the published judgment [para 43]. Alternative statutory interest also addressed [para 54].
- **Counterclaim:** none (defendants did not appear).

### Debtor's defences and complaints
No defence was filed. Counsel for the claimant put the points the defendants might have taken [paras 4, 6]:
1. **Guarantee void for past consideration** (Procurement Agreement entered shortly before the Guarantee) [para 11].
2. **Guarantors discharged by variation** (five amendments to the Procurement Agreement, including extension of term) [paras 13, 14].
3. **Procurement fee is a penalty** [para 37].
4. **Procurement fees not covered by the 9 November 2017 demand** (they fell due from 1 June 2018, after the demand and after issue on 1 February 2018) [paras 36, 37, 40].
5. **15% Default Rate interest is a penalty** [para 44].
6. **Interest not covered by the demand**, split into interest accrued to the demand, to issue, to judgment and after judgment [para 44].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected:** the guarantee was requested and understood to be part of the bargain when the Procurement Agreement was made, satisfying the Chitty exception for prior acts [para 12].
2. **Rejected:** cl 2.9 preserved liability despite amendments, and the guarantors signed and ratified each amendment [para 15].
3. **Rejected:** the fee is a primary obligation forming part of the price for services, not a secondary obligation on breach [para 39].
4. **Upheld:** cl 2.2 required the demand to set out the calculation; no fees were due at the date of the demand, so no obligation to pay them was triggered. A fresh demand remains open [paras 41, 55(d)].
5. **Rejected:** 15% was not out of all proportion to LGT's legitimate interest given its large upfront outlays and cash flow exposure [paras 45, 46].
6. **Partly upheld:** interest accrued up to the date of the demand was validly demanded (a demand coupled with an offer to accept less); interest accruing after the demand, before or after issue, was not demanded and judgment was not given for it in the defendants' absence [paras 50, 55(c), (g)].

### Money result
- Principal: **US$2,300,170** to the claimant [para 55(b)].
- Contractual interest at 15% from each due date to the date of demand, per Schedule 4 (figure not reproduced in the judgment text) [paras 43, 55(c)].
- Legal and other expenses: US$17,432.34 and £16,161.85 allowed [paras 53, 55(f)].
- Procurement fees (US$234,841.65) and interest on them: refused for want of demand [para 55(d), (e)].
- Statutory interest from due dates to judgment at **US$ LIBOR plus 1%** (statute not named), or alternatively the claimant may serve a new demand for further contractual interest; election left to the claimant [paras 54, 55(h)].
- Costs: not stated. Order to be drawn up to give effect to the judgment [para 56].

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* What decided the case was paperwork: signed guarantee and amendments, the schedule of 18 invoices, the cl 2.3 certificate (prima facie evidence) and a demand letter that set out the calculation. Where the demand did not compute a sum (future fees, future interest), the claim failed even against absent defendants.
- *Commentary:* Collections lesson: when a contract requires a demand "setting forth" the amount, every head (principal, accrued interest, fees, costs) must be quantified in the demand, and further demands served as new sums accrue. A reservation of rights is not a demand.
- *Commentary:* Challenge should still test an undefended file: it should raise but reject past consideration, discharge by variation and penalty arguments where signed ratifications and a primary-obligation fee exist, and uphold the "not demanded" objection for any sum the demand did not calculate.
- *Commentary:* Reconciliation should apply post-demand payments to specific invoices (10148 part, 10160 whole) and compute 15% contractual interest only to the demand date, with a separate statutory-interest scenario at the court-chosen rate for the later period.
- *Commentary:* Resolution should produce a court-claim pack for principal, demanded interest and expenses, and a fresh written demand (with certificate) for procurement fees and post-demand interest before any further claim.
