# CEP Holdings Ltd and CEP Claddings Ltd v Steni AS — [2009] EWHC 2447 (QB)
- **Court / venue:** High Court of Justice, Queen's Bench Division, Royal Courts of Justice, Strand, London WC2A 2LL
- **Judge:** Mrs Justice Gloster DBE
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Hearing 11-15 May, 18-22 May and 8 June 2009 (further written submissions 14, 15 and 17 June 2009); judgment 9 October 2009
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/qb/2009/2447
- **Tier:** core: supplier's counterclaim for unpaid invoices (EUR 216,900.52 admitted subject to set-off, plus EUR 18,508 withheld over a quality complaint); the buyer's withholding failed because it did not inspect on receipt and settled with its own customer without involving the supplier.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Creditor (Defendant / Part 20 Claimant):** Steni AS, Norwegian manufacturer of cladding panels (successor by merger of 30 June 2004 to Steni Norden AS, the supplying entity) [para 3].
- **Debtors (Claimants / Part 20 Defendants):** CEP Holdings Ltd ("Holdings"), Steni's exclusive UK/Ireland distributor under the Exclusive Distributor Agreement ("EDA") of 26 February 1999; CEP Claddings Ltd ("Claddings"), the Holdings subsidiary that purchased and was invoiced for products from about April 2002 [paras 1, 25].
- Downstream: Joyners PA Ltd (subcontractor, Claddings' customer) and Countryside Properties plc (main contractor) on the Aberfeldy project, Tower Hamlets [para 108].

### Amounts
- Unpaid invoices (counterclaim against Claddings): EUR 216,900.52 plus interest; admitted subject to set-off [paras 17, 169].
- Aberfeldy sums withheld: EUR 18,508 plus interest [paras 18, 125].
- Steni's damages counterclaim against Holdings (breach of cl 2.3): expert scenarios c. GBP 314,000 / 84,000 / 160,000; Claimants' expert GBP 66,294 [paras 170-173].
- Claimants' own claims (relied on as set-off): damages for wrongful termination of the EDA (quantum not determined) and an overcharging claim under cl 3.2 for 2002-2005 [paras 7-14, 19].
- Interest: claimed "plus interest"; basis not stated.

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **Set-off against damages for wrongful termination** of the EDA (Termination Notice 10 Mar 2005; treated as repudiation and accepted by letter of 7 Apr 2005) [paras 11, 17].
2. **Overcharging under cl 3.2** (annual price increases 2002-2005 said not to reflect production and raw-material cost movements) [para 14].
3. **Quality: scratch resistance.** Complaint from Joyners in early July 2003 (letter 14 Jul 2003); contract placed c. 18 Feb 2003 [paras 109, 120].
4. **Quality: thickness below tolerance**, first raised by Countryside at the site meeting on 31 Jul 2003 (14 boards measured c. 24 Jul 2003), months after delivery and after panels had been cut and mounted; relied on to withhold EUR 18,508 including the Joyners settlement cost [paras 118-121, 125].
5. **No breach of cl 4.6 / 4.7**: claim report supplied 11 Jan 2005 within the cure period; the Joyners compromise was not a more favourable guarantee term [paras 105, 127].
6. **Novation/assignment** of the EDA to Claddings (circular letter of 4 Jun 2001) [paras 13, 25].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected.** Steni was entitled to terminate because Holdings failed to use all reasonable endeavours under cl 2.3 and did not cure within 30 days [para 102]; and also because non-payment for the Aberfeldy products breached cl 3.4 [para 136]. The wrongful-termination claim failed, so there was nothing to set off [paras 161, 169, 176].
2. **Rejected.** Each annual price revision had been mutually agreed and any reservation of rights was waived or abandoned [paras 157-160].
3. **Rejected.** Steni's internal and external tests showed the panels met the scratch-resistance specification; poor handling on site was the likely cause [para 117].
4. **Partly upheld (breach) but rejected as a justification for withholding.** White panels were up to 0.2 mm under specification, a breach of the cl 6.1 guarantee even though fit for purpose [para 118]. But the defect was detectable on receipt, Claddings failed to inspect under cl 6.6, Steni was never asked to replace panels, and the Claimants deliberately excluded Steni from the Joyners compromise; liability was limited by cl 6.5 to replacing unmounted non-compliant panels and fixing/removal costs were not recoverable [paras 131-135]. The grey panels were never alleged defective, so EUR 1,401.88 could not be withheld on any view, and the white panels still had to be paid for [para 135].
5. **Upheld in part.** The cl 4.6 breach was technical and remedied in time, so not a valid termination ground [paras 105-106]; the compromise was not a breach of cl 4.7 [para 137]. Neither point affected the debt.
6. **Rejected.** The court found against the Claimants on novation/assignment; the circular letter was found not to have been received by Steni [paras 25, 138].

### Money result
- Claddings liable to Steni for EUR 216,900.52 plus interest (unpaid invoices) [para 169] and EUR 18,508 plus interest (Aberfeldy) [para 168].
- Holdings liable to Steni for GBP 125,000 damages for breach of cl 2.3 (Scenario 3 discounted; "rough and ready") [paras 174-175].
- Claimants' claim dismissed; no set-off [para 176].
- Interest: awarded "plus interest" but rate, basis and arithmetic not stated. Costs: not stated. Form of order: not stated.

## Commentary
*(Commentary: author's analysis, not the court's.)*
- The decisive evidence on the EUR 18,508 was the contractual inspect-on-receipt clause (cl 6.6) combined with the finding that a simple measurement before dispatch would have revealed the thickness defect [paras 119, 133, 135]. A genuine specification breach did not justify withholding the price where the contract limited the remedy to replacement and the buyer never asked for replacement.
- Settling with your own customer without the supplier's agreement is risky: the court held the Claimants had "only themselves to blame" when the compromise proved irrecoverable [para 133]. Collections teams facing a pass-through set-off should check whether the supplier was invited to agree the downstream settlement.
- The bulk of the debt (EUR 216,900.52) was admitted subject to set-off; once the cross-claim failed, the debt followed automatically. Reconciliation should split admitted debt from the disputed balance and treat the admitted part as payable regardless of the cross-claim.
- DisputeOS mapping: Challenge should accept the thickness non-conformity as a real breach but rebut the withholding, because notice came months after delivery and after cutting/mounting, with no inspection and no replacement request; it should reject the scratch complaint on the test evidence. Reconciliation should match the EUR 18,510.54 breakdown to the EUR 18,508 withheld and flag the grey-panel element (EUR 1,401.88) as having no defect basis at all. Resolution: court-claim pack for EUR 216,900.52 plus EUR 18,508 with interest from the cl 3.4 due dates (30 days from end of month of shipment); at most offer replacement of unmounted non-compliant panels, not a credit note for site costs.
- Caveat: the case is primarily a distributorship termination dispute; the GBP 125,000 reasonable-endeavours damages should not be treated as part of an invoice-recovery workflow.
