# Pharos Offshore Group Ltd v Keynvor Morlift Ltd — [2025] EWHC 1764 (TCC)

- **Court / venue:** High Court, Business and Property Courts, Technology and Construction Court (KBD), Rolls Building, Fetter Lane, London EC4A 1NL
- **Judge:** Mr Justice Constable
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Heard 9 to 12 and 19 June 2025; handed down remotely, deemed 10.30 on 11 July 2025
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/tcc/2025/1764
- **Tier:** core: equipment-and-crew hire supplier sues for six invoices; the buyer disputed the day-rate basis, waiting-on-weather charges and mobilisation delay, and counterclaimed for vessel spread costs and liquidated damages; mixed result with a partial set-off.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Creditor / claimant:** Pharos Offshore Group Ltd, provider of subsea equipment and offshore personnel; supplied the UTV-670 jet trenching vehicle, ancillary kit and operators ("Seller") [paras 2, 60].
- **Debtor / defendant:** Keynvor Morlift Ltd (KML), marine contractor subcontracted by Prysmian to bury the Viking Link cable off Jutland ("Buyer") [paras 2, 60].

### Amounts
- Invoiced: £918,471.79 incl VAT; paid £135,048; balance claimed with statutory interest [para 5].
- KML's case: maximum entitlement £274,742.05 ex VAT before set-off [paras 5, 188].
- Counterclaim: £1,217,806.86 to £1,434,049.50 for additional vessel/spread hire and harbour fees, and/or liquidated damages of £185,280 [para 5].
- Interest: Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998, applicability admitted in principle by KML [para 227].

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **Daily charges capped at 10 days** (fixed and firm price, PO supersedes negotiations, no oral modification) [para 64]. Raised as a construction point only at trial; not in the Defence [para 66].
2. **No payment for waiting on weather**, because the Revised PO (25 May 2022) deliberately removed the words and the parties agreed this orally [paras 82, 83].
3. **Late mobilisation:** equipment should have arrived by 9 June 2022 (date notified 31 May 2022); customs delay 6 to 17 June was Pharos's fault [paras 95, 101, 110, 111].
4. **Liquidated damages** under cl 8.3 for late completion [paras 90, 91].
5. **Equipment unfit / not to specification:** UTV-670 unsuitable for nearshore slopes over 15°; lay-flat hoses and defective couplings; sensor, pressure, traction, hose and HPU failures; failure to advise starting offshore [paras 142, 145, 165]. Complaints contemporaneous in the DPRs during June to August 2022; timing of any written notice under cl 12.1 not stated.
6. **Quantum challenges** to personnel, expenses, flights and transit charges [paras 187 to 204].
7. **Counterclaim** for delay costs (vessel spread) [para 207].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected**: "10" was an indicative duration tied to an "indicative programme"; Pharos was entitled to day rates for a reasonable time, not capped at 10 days [para 67].
2. **Rejected**: no oral agreement existed (Mr Scullion admitted no such conversation); the words were overwritten unintentionally and the weather risk allocation was unchanged [paras 86 to 89].
3. **Rejected**: 9 June was not a contractual date and no implied mechanism fixed one; the MBES survey was delayed by weather until 23/24 June, so arrival on 20 June caused no delay; Pharos could not reasonably have shipped before 6 June [paras 98, 105 to 108, 112, 113].
4. **Rejected**: no date was specified in either PO and time was a reasonable-time obligation, so cl 8.3 never bit [paras 92 to 94].
5. **Partly upheld**: the UTV-670 itself was not unfit (KML's own expert did not say so) and the sequencing allegation failed [paras 156, 163]; but the couplings were sub-standard (conceded) and 113 hours of technical downtime against 166 hours of trenching was about five times the expected maintenance allowance and was Pharos's responsibility [paras 145, 166, 167]. Overall 8 days of culpable delay (works should have demobilised 9 August, not 17 August 2022) [paras 178, 180, 183].
6. **Partly upheld**: allowed: equipment spread 51 days £216,750; personnel £299,000; transport £21,903; additional equipment £33,650.76; flights £24,639.16 (5% not 10% uplift). Disallowed: £34,000 transit, personnel before 19 June, daily expenses and unproven flights [paras 192 to 204].
7. **Partly upheld**: 8 days at a recoverable average of £14,151.83 per day = £113,214.63; KML's internal personnel costs (SAGE extract), harbour fees, USBL pole and trailer winch disallowed for want of evidence [paras 208 to 224].

### Money result
- Pharos's entitlement on the disputed invoices: £595,942.76 ex VAT, less £88,540 ex VAT paid = £507,402.76 plus VAT, i.e. £608,883.31 incl VAT [paras 205, 206, 225].
- KML's counterclaim: £113,214.63, which may be set off [para 226]. Net figure after set-off is not stated in the judgment (arithmetically £608,883.31 less £113,214.63 = £495,668.68, but how VAT interacts with the set-off is not addressed).
- Interest: Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 applies in principle; amount to be agreed, failing which the court will decide [para 227]. Costs: not stated.

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* Contemporaneous third-party records won the timing argument. Prysmian's Daily Call notes proved the survey was weather-bound, so the customs delay caused no loss. Collections teams should keep the customer's own or the end-client's progress logs, not just internal records.
- *Commentary:* The buyer's own PO wording ("indicative programme") defeated its cap argument, and an unexplained deletion in a revised PO did not shift the weather risk. A statement of truth with no witness behind it badly damaged KML's credibility.
- *Commentary:* The supplier still lost about 19% of its recovered value to set-off because its ancillary kit failed far more than its own maintenance allowance predicted. Quantification was disciplined both ways: unexplained invoice lines (the £34,000 transit, expenses) and unexplained counterclaim lines (SAGE ledger entries) both failed for lack of evidence.
- *Commentary:* For DisputeOS: Reconciliation should rebuild entitlement from contract rates times reasonable duration (51 days equipment, 52 days personnel), match payments to specific invoices, and drop invoice lines with no contractual basis. Challenge should reject the fixed-duration and late-mobilisation defences but uphold the equipment-quality complaint, since the DPRs recorded failures as they happened. Resolution should treat this as a settlement-offer case (net roughly £495k plus 1998 Act interest on the debt element) rather than a pure court-claim pack; a partial credit note for the unsupported transit charge would be appropriate.
