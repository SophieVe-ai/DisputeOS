# KSY Juice Blends UK Ltd v Citrosuco GmbH — [2024] EWHC 2098 (Comm)

- **Court / venue:** High Court, Business and Property Courts of England and Wales, London Circuit Commercial Court (KBD)
- **Judge:** His Honour Judge Pearce, sitting as a Judge of the High Court
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Heard 5 to 8 March 2024; handed down remotely 10.30am, Friday 9 August 2024
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/comm/2024/2098
- **Tier:** core: seller invoiced a buyer for goods the buyer never called off, at a placeholder "invoicing price", and claimed about €4.8m; the court held that the volume above the fixed tranche was an unenforceable agreement to agree on price, so the invoices created no debt. A clear "invoice without an agreed price or delivery instruction" failure.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Creditor / claimant:** KSY Juice Blends UK Ltd (described as a Greek company; part of the Orange Be Global group), seller of orange pulp wash ("wesos") [paras 1, Appendix 1].
- **Debtor / defendant:** Citrosuco GmbH (described as a Brazilian company; one of the three major FCOJ producers), buyer [paras 1, 7].

### Amounts
- Claim at its highest: around €4.8 million (price, alternatively damages for non-acceptance and post-termination loss) [para 1].
- Admitted unpaid: €64,287.72 for the 42 MT of 2020 wesos delivered [paras 2, 3].
- Cross-claim by Citrosuco: US$240,112.42, admitted by KSY [paras 3, 4]. Its subject matter is not described in the judgment beyond references to sums or receivables KSY owed Citrosuco [Appendix 2].
- Interest claimed: not stated.

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **Agreement to agree:** the 800 MT/year "open price" tranche was unenforceable because no price was agreed by December of the previous year [paras 21(a), 114]. Raised in writing by 12 December 2019 internally, and to KSY on 13 January 2020 and 15 May 2020 ("no price agreed and we decided not to take it") [Appendix 2].
2. **No contractual price at €1,600/MT:** the invoicing price was not the true price; no implied reasonable or market price; no implied duty to use reasonable endeavours to agree [paras 113 to 126].
3. **No claim for the price before termination:** no delivery instruction given, property had not passed, and s.49 SGA 1979 does not apply [para 135].
4. **No damages for non-acceptance:** an available market exists, so damages are limited to the contract-market difference [para 138].
5. **Termination wrongful:** KSY's 25 September 2020 termination was itself a repudiation [para 19].
6. **Set-off:** admitted €64,287.72 exceeded by KSY's liability on the US$240,112.42 cross-claim; 2020 invoices put on hold for netting from March 2020 [para 3; Appendix 2].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Upheld**: although the parties intended to deal in 3,600 MT, "open" price contrasted with the "fixed" price and could only mean a price to be agreed; the 800 MT/year tranche was an unenforceable agreement to agree, while the 400 MT tranche survived [paras 114, 118, 128, 145(a)].
2. **Upheld**: €1,600/MT was an invoicing figure adjusted by free trucks, not the price; implying it as a fallback would have removed the seller's incentive to negotiate; reasonable or market price terms were too uncertain; severance did not help [paras 115 to 127].
3. **Upheld**: even with a price, the contract required a buyer's delivery instruction (cl 9); issuing invoices did not trigger payment, and s.49(2) did not apply [para 136].
4. **Rejected (obiter)**: had there been a contract, there was no available market and KSY, unable to resell, would have recovered the contract price less avoidable delivery costs, with no reduction for failure to mitigate [paras 139, 140].
5. **Upheld**: with no binding contract for the 800 MT, KSY was not entitled to terminate. Obiter: otherwise the 30 July 2020 letter would have been a repudiation validly accepted [paras 141, 145(e)].
6. **Not decided as such**: both sides admitted the two sums (€64,287.72 owed by Citrosuco; US$240,112.42 owed by KSY) [paras 3, 4].

### Money result
- KSY's claim for the price or damages on the 800 MT tranches fails [paras 128, 145].
- Admitted cross-liabilities remain: €64,287.72 to KSY and US$240,112.42 to Citrosuco. The judgment does not state a net figure, currency conversion or form of order.
- Interest: not stated. Costs: to be dealt with consequentially, including whether to reflect non-compliance with PD 57AC in the witness statements [para 23].

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* The decisive document was the contract itself: "fixed" against "open" price, a separate "invoicing price", and a buyer call-off mechanism. The seller's own emails ("once agreed we will adjust the difference"; "we can issue a credit note accordingly") showed it knew its invoices were placeholders.
- *Commentary:* Collections lesson: an invoice is not a debt unless the contract makes it one. Where price is left to be agreed and delivery is on buyer call-off, invoicing unordered and unpriced goods (and factoring those invoices) creates exposure, not receivables. Get price agreements signed in writing before production, as the entire-agreement clause required.
- *Commentary:* For DisputeOS: Evidence should classify these invoices as unsupported (no delivery note or call-off, no agreed price). Challenge should uphold the "no agreed price / no call-off" defence, noting it was raised in writing within weeks (13 January 2020). Reconciliation should net the admitted €64,287.72 against the admitted US$240,112.42 cross-claim, flagging the currency mismatch. Resolution should recommend a credit note cancelling the 2019 variable-volume invoices, with no letter before action on them, and a settlement netting the admitted balances.
