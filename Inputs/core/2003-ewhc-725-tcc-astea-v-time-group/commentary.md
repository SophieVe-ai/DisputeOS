# Astea (UK) Ltd v Time Group Ltd — [2003] EWHC 725 (TCC)
- **Court / venue:** High Court, Queen's Bench Division, Technology and Construction Court, St Dunstan's House, 133-137 Fetter Lane, London EC4A 1HD
- **Judge:** HH Judge Richard Seymour QC
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Judgment 9 April 2003; hearing dates not stated
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/tcc/2003/725
- **Tier:** core: software supplier sues for unpaid licence-fee instalments and services invoices; customer says delay was a repudiatory breach it accepted and counterclaims wasted costs and lost staff savings. Clear example of a "delay" defence failing for lack of evidence and of a customer's change of mind dressed up as a performance complaint.

## Inputs
### Parties
- **Creditor / claimant:** Astea (UK) Ltd, supplier and licensor of "ServiceAlliance" field-service software and implementation services; about twenty employees [para 1].
- **Debtor / defendant (Part 20 claimant):** Time Group Ltd, manufacturer and retailer of personal computers, licensee and buyer of services [paras 5, 10].
- **Non-party:** Lynx Commercial Systems Ltd (supplier of Time's CS/3 accounting package, with which the software had to be integrated), in separate dispute with Time [paras 8, 78].

### Amounts
- **Invoiced:** licence fees £170,400 plus VAT; services £85,382.04 inc VAT [paras 14, 29].
- **Paid:** £56,800 plus VAT (first licence instalment) [para 33].
- **Claimed at issue:** £218,862.04 (£133,480 licence balance inc VAT; £85,382.04 services) [para 29].
- **Claimed at trial:** £214,769.14, after Astea capped services time at the £60,000 Schedule C value: £133,480 licence balance; £70,500 time (£60,000 + VAT); £10,789.14 expenses (£9,182.25 + VAT). Figures not disputed as figures [para 29].
- **Counterclaim (Part 20):** repayment of £56,800 plus VAT; Lynx integration cost £89,417.50; management costs £22,307.54; hardware £3,700; lost staff savings £48,330 (October 2000 to March 2001) continuing at £12,082 per month to December 2002 [paras 33, 167].
- **Interest claimed:** contractual late payment charge 1.5% per month (cl 6.3) is in the contract; basis of interest actually claimed not stated [paras 21, 174].

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **Repudiation by delay, accepted on 6 March 2001:** Astea failed to complete implementation by 1 August 2000 (cl 15.5), by the end of October / 6 November 2000 (alleged variation by email of 17 October 2000), or within a reasonable time (13 November 2000, end December 2000 or 6 March 2001). Payment complaints raised in writing only after the statutory demand (solicitors' letter 7 June 2001) [paras 9 to 13 (pleading), 30, 31, 136].
2. **Single agreement, so repudiation ended the obligation to pay the licence balance** (Astea argued two or three separate agreements) [paras 11, 30].
3. **Services overcharged:** cost would have been £42,647.83 with reasonable skill and care; in closing narrowed to disallowing charges after 13 November 2000 (£12,848.77 inc VAT) [para 32].
4. **Implied terms of satisfactory quality and fitness (s 14 Sale of Goods Act 1979)** [para 30].
5. **Total failure of consideration** for sums paid and claimed [para 30].
6. **Set-off of Part 20 damages** [para 32].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected:** the 1 August 2000 date was a "scheduled" target (reasonable endeavours) and was waived; the 17 October 2000 email did not vary the contract to a fixed date; Time failed to prove a reasonable time had been exceeded (no expert evidence; Time itself was not ready before 30 October 2000; testing was in Time's control). Even if breach, not repudiatory, and not accepted: the project was put on hold because Time's managing director changed his mind about paying, not because of delay [paras 158, 159, 161 to 164].
2. **Upheld on the point but irrelevant:** the contract was a single agreement (Astea's two-or-three-contracts argument was "a triumph of analysis over reality"), but with no accepted repudiation the licence balance remained payable [paras 157, 158, 165].
3. **Rejected:** no attempt to justify £42,647.83; invoices accepted as accurate and the post-13 November charges stand, since the claim was allowed as modified [paras 32, 56, 165].
4. **Not needed:** no suggestion the software was defective or the services incompetent [para 173]. Astea's exclusion and cap clauses (cl 11.1, 11.2) would have failed UCTA reasonableness for want of evidence [para 34].
5. **Rejected:** follows from no accepted repudiation [para 165].
6. **Rejected:** Part 20 claim dismissed; in any event no loss proved (Lynx figure "made up" in the witness box, management costs unsupported, hardware unidentified, staff savings "illusory") and Time failed to mitigate by letting Astea finish [paras 166 to 173].

### Money result
- Judgment for Astea for **£214,769.14** [para 174].
- Interest: to be argued ("as to which I will hear Counsel"); rate and basis not decided in this judgment [para 174].
- Time's Part 20 claim dismissed [para 174].
- Costs: not stated.

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* What decided the case was the burden of proof on delay. The customer alleged slow performance but brought no expert evidence, relied on the supplier's own invoices, and was itself not ready for the integration work. A "reasonable time" defence needs evidence of what a reasonable time was.
- *Commentary:* Internal emails from the debtor ("we must pay it before it affects the implementation") and the timing of the first written complaint (after the statutory demand) showed the real reason for non-payment. Collections teams should note when a complaint first appears relative to the chase history.
- *Commentary:* Challenge should treat implementation dates described as "scheduled" or conditional on customer co-operation as targets, and should only uphold a delay defence where the debtor shows the delay was the supplier's fault and quantifies it; it should reject a repudiation argument where the debtor allowed work to continue after the alleged deadline.
- *Commentary:* Reconciliation should reflect the creditor's own concession: services time capped at the contract value (£60,000 plus VAT) even though more was invoiced. Where the contract states a "Total Value of Services", flag invoices that exceed it.
- *Commentary:* Resolution: a court-claim pack was appropriate after the statutory demand drew only a delay story. Contractual interest at 1.5% per month (cl 6.3) is available in principle, but the judgment left interest open, so DisputeOS should show it as a claimable head rather than a decided figure.
