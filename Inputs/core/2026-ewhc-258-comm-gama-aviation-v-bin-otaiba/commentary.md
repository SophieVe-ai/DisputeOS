# Gama Aviation FZC v Bin Otaiba Investment Group — [2026] EWHC 258 (Comm)

- **Court / venue:** High Court, King's Bench Division, Business and Property Courts, London Circuit Commercial Court, Rolls Building, Fetter Lane, London EC4A 1NL
- **Judge:** Rosalind Phelps KC, sitting as a Deputy High Court Judge
- **Hearing / hand-down:** Heard 19 and 20 January 2026 (defendant absent); draft circulated 10 February 2026; handed down remotely 10.30am, 24 February 2026
- **Source:** https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/comm/2026/258
- **Tier:** core: aircraft manager claims about US$3.9m of unpaid management fees and recharged maintenance and hangarage invoices plus compounded contractual interest; the owner alleged invalid termination, unapproved work, inadequate invoices, excessive parking and a settlement, and counterclaimed for detention. Caveat: the defendant did not appear and has signalled a CPR 39.3 set-aside application.

- **Creditor / claimant:** Gama Aviation FZC (GAF), aircraft manager (supplier of management services and recharger of third-party maintenance costs) [paras 1, 2].

- **Related third party:** Gama Aviation (Engineering) Ltd (GAEL), GAF's sister company, which did the maintenance at Bournemouth [para 2].

### Amounts
- Claimed: net US$3,892,154.73 (all invoices 16 November 2020 to 15 December 2025 less payments), as debt or damages [para 124].
- Hangarage component pleaded: US$1,597,164.67 [para 116].
- Contractual interest (clause 6.4, GAF limiting itself to 1% per month compounded): US$1,776,414.64 at 15 December 2025, continuing [para 127].
- Counterclaim: unquantified (alternative charter costs, lost charter income, recommissioning costs, depreciation) [paras 136, 138].
- Disputed "unapproved" maintenance: US$1,787,046.28 across four invoices [para 96].

### Debtor's defences and complaints
1. **Termination:** the 24 March 2022 email terminated the agreement (effective about 22/23 September 2022), so later fees are not due; alternatively GAF accepted termination by its 19 July 2022 email [paras 77, 84].
2. **No approval for emergent/unscheduled work** (cl 5.2), covering invoices GME13943, 14149, 14163, 14233; GME13943 approved only verbally by unauthorised persons [para 96]. No itemised challenge was ever made to the status reports sent from April 2021 [paras 104, 106].
3. **Invoices lacked detail and supporting documents** (Sch 2 "invoicing mechanism") [para 108].
4. **No budget plan / no warning of higher costs** before the five-year inspection [paras 111, 113].
5. **Parking:** hangar at £957/day imposed unilaterally instead of outdoor parking at around US$100 to US$200/day; no evidence it was in a hangar [para 117]. Charges were notified 18 and 24 May 2021, 5 August 2021 and August 2022 [para 119].
6. **Settlement:** the US$711,408.25 proposal of 6 February 2023 was an agreed settlement [para 122].
7. **Lien invalid:** no lien after termination; costs not properly incurred; no fixed amount; GAEL, not GAF, has possession [para 132].
8. **Counterclaim:** aircraft wrongfully detained [para 136].

## Outcome
### Decision on each defence
1. **Rejected**: cl 10.1 requirements (six months' notice, expiry date) and cl 11.8 (confirmatory mail copy) were conditions precedent; the email purported to terminate immediately; GAF's 30 May and 19 July 2022 emails were not acceptances, and BOIG kept approving CCOs. BOIG would in any event have been estopped by convention [paras 82 to 86].
2. **Rejected**: this was scheduled maintenance under cl 5.1, not cl 5.2 unscheduled work; airworthiness repairs needed no further approval; costs were recoverable under cl 6.2 if reasonable; BOIG never identified any unapproved or unreasonable item; Mohammed El Masaoudi had at least apparent authority, and GME13943 was part-paid [paras 98 to 107].
3. **Rejected**: the Sch 2 documentation requirement applied only to "Fees", and in any case invoices cross-referred to GAEL invoices, CCOs and status reports [paras 109, 110].
4. **Rejected**: quarterly budgets were required, but estimates were given; the original quote excluded emergent work; cost growth came from no pre-purchase inspection and BOIG's slow approvals; budgets were not a condition precedent to payment [paras 112 to 115].
5. **Partly upheld (in effect)**: hangarage was justified (coastal environment, best practice) and repeatedly notified, but GAF itself conceded an adjustment: 75% of days at the hangar rate and 25% at the outdoor rate [paras 119 to 121].
6. **Rejected**: "hopeless": not clearly an offer to settle, and GAF plainly did not accept it [para 122].
7. **Rejected**: lien survives termination under cl 10.7; it does not need to be for a fixed amount; GAF has constructive possession through GAEL and cl 7.1 dispenses with physical possession [para 133].
8. **Rejected**: delay was caused by BOIG's non-engagement and non-payment; cl 4.2 anticipated maintenance unavailability; GAF was entitled to the lien; losses were unparticularised; charter income was excluded (cl 8 deleted) [paras 137 to 139].

### Money result
- Judgment for GAF on Annex A (US$3,892,154.73) less two adjustments: (a) parking re-priced at 75% hangar / 25% outdoor; (b) a credit of about £17,000 for over-billing found on final reconciliation of estimated against actual costs [paras 120, 125, 126].
- Contractual interest under cl 6.4 at 1% per month compounded monthly (Annex B, US$1,776,414.64 at 15 December 2025), reduced to reflect the same adjustments [paras 127 to 129].
- Declarations that GAF validly exercised its contractual lien and may sell the aircraft if BOIG fails to pay within the time ordered [paras 134, 135].
- Counterclaim dismissed [para 140].
- Adjusted sums, the form of order and the final form of declaration were left for further submissions; final figures not stated [para 141]. Costs not stated. BOIG signalled a CPR 39.3 set-aside application [paras 144 to 151].

## Commentary
- *Commentary:* The decisive evidence was the creditor's disciplined reporting: monthly management reports, CCOs, 27 status reports, statements of account and itemised current status reports, none of which the debtor queried at the time, plus the debtor's own consultant (JSSI) finding no fault with scope or cost.
- *Commentary:* Collections lesson: "you didn't get approval" fails where the contract gives the manager authority for necessary work and the debtor never itemises what it disputes. Equally, the creditor volunteered the hangar/apron split and the £17,000 reconciliation credit, which supported its credibility in an undefended trial.
- *Commentary:* An informal "we terminate" email did not satisfy a notice clause requiring a notice period and postal confirmation. Reconciliation should keep accruing management fees after a defective termination, but Challenge should flag the risk if the creditor's own later emails could look like acceptance.
- *Commentary:* For DisputeOS: Reconciliation should net invoices against the three payments, apply the conceded parking re-rate and the reconciliation credit, then compute compound interest at 1% per month (the contract's "one per cent (3%)" ambiguity resolved by the creditor choosing the lower rate). Challenge should reject the approval, documentation, budget and settlement defences but uphold the parking quantum point. Resolution: court-claim pack with lien-enforcement declarations, plus a credit note for the £17,000 over-billing; given the possible set-aside application, keep proof of service of trial notices on file.
